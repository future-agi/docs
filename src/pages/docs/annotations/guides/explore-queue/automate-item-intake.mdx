---
title: "Automate item intake"
description: "Set a rule that keeps feeding the queue on its own, then trigger it once to see what it catches"
---

A rule checks new candidates against conditions you set and adds the matches to `Support quality review` on its own, so nobody has to go looking for fresh items to hand out. This walks through building one, running it once to see what it catches, and what actually happens behind that Trigger button.

## Create a rule

From `Support quality review`'s **Rules** tab, start a new rule. Give it a name, add the conditions that decide which candidates should match, and set how often it runs: **Manual**, **Hourly**, **Daily**, **Weekly**, or **Monthly**. Manual means the rule never fires on its own, someone has to trigger it every time; the other four put it on a recurring schedule instead. Save it, and it's ready to evaluate.

<Note>
Once a rule exists you can go back and change its name, conditions, or frequency, or remove it, the same way you created it.
</Note>

## Run it once before you trust it

Whatever frequency you picked, trigger the rule yourself to see what it catches against the queue's current items right now, before you let it run unattended. This works the same on a Manual rule, where it's the only way the rule ever fires, and on a scheduled one, where it's a quick way to sanity-check the conditions before the schedule takes over.

## How and when a rule runs

This is the part that trips people up, so be precise about it:

- A **scheduled** rule (Hourly, Daily, Weekly, Monthly) doesn't fire at the exact minute its frequency implies. The platform checks for rules that are due roughly every 30 minutes, so treat the frequency as "within about half an hour of," not "on the dot."
- **Triggering a rule yourself** doesn't block the page. The run is dispatched off your click and the request comes back immediately, while the actual matching happens in the background.
- When a triggered run finishes, **you** (whoever ran it) and **every manager on the queue** get an email, whether you triggered it by hand or it fired on schedule.

## New items don't arrive silently

Even without that email, annotators find out. New-item digest emails go out on two cadences: a realtime one roughly every fifteen minutes, or a daily one that lands at each person's own local digest hour. Whatever a rule adds to `Support quality review` shows up in both, so a rule firing while you're not watching still reaches the people who need to work the items.
