Access on Future AGI is decided in two places, not one. Where the plan and the people live is one boundary; where the work lives is another. Almost every access question that reaches support turns out to be someone holding the right role at the wrong level.

## What is access control on Future AGI?

Every account has an **organization**, and inside it one or more **workspaces**. You hold a role in each:

- Your **organization role** is one of Owner, Admin, Member or Viewer. It decides who you can manage, and whether you reach organization settings and billing
- Your **workspace role** is one of Workspace Admin, Workspace Member or Workspace Viewer. It decides what you can do with the work inside that workspace

The two are independent. An organization Viewer added to a workspace as a Workspace Member can create, edit and share everything in it.

Owners and Admins are Workspace Admins in every workspace automatically. Everyone else sees only the workspaces they were added to.

## Before you start

Access control is available on every deployment. Only an Owner or an Admin can change organization roles, create workspaces, or open the Members page.

A Workspace Admin can invite into their own workspace and manage its members, without reaching billing or anyone else's workspace.

## Where it shows up

Access lives in **Settings**, across three surfaces.

- **Members:** everyone in the organization with their role, their workspaces, and whether they are Active or Pending
- **Workspaces:** every workspace with its admins, start date and last update
- **Your Workspaces:** each workspace you belong to, and the tabs inside it that your workspace role allows

The workspace switcher at the top of the left sidebar is the fourth surface, and the busiest one. It creates workspaces, moves you between workspaces and organizations, opens Settings, and carries **Invite members** for a Workspace Admin.

## What you can use it for

- **Keep one team's work out of another's**, for example "the research workspace must not see the customer workspace's traces"
- **Give someone control of one team and nothing else**, for example "a lead who runs their own workspace but never touches billing"
- **Let someone read without changing anything**, for example "a stakeholder who reviews evaluation results and cannot edit them"
- **Show one thing to somebody outside the organization**, for example "a dataset a customer needs to see without an account"

## Roles vs share links

Roles decide what someone with an account can reach. A [share link](/docs/roles-and-permissions/guides/share-outside-your-organization) grants access to one resource without granting a role at all, and a dataset link works with no account and no sign-in. The two are separate systems, and a workspace boundary does not constrain a link somebody has already sent, so anyone auditing access has to look at both.
