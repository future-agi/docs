## Two roles, two jobs

Everyone gets two roles, and they do different jobs.

- Your **organization role** decides who you can manage, and whether you reach organization settings and billing
- Your **workspace role** decides what you can do with the work inside a workspace

The two are independent, which is what makes the useful combinations possible. A team lead can run one workspace completely while holding a low organization role, and an engineer can build freely in their workspace without reaching anything organization-wide.

## Organization roles

Everyone in an organization holds one of four roles. They rank in this order, Owner highest and Viewer lowest. The ordering decides who can invite and manage whom.

These are the descriptions the product itself shows you when you set someone's role.

An organization keeps at least one Owner at all times, so ownership moves by promoting a second Owner and then demoting the first. An Admin reaches billing and the plan on the same terms as an Owner, but cannot change an Owner or another Admin.

<Note>
Owners and Admins are **Workspace Admins** in every workspace, automatically.

- They need no workspace membership of their own
- Promoting someone to Admin or Owner grants that access across every workspace at once
- Demoting them asks you to pick the workspaces they keep
</Note>

## Workspace roles

A Member or Viewer sees nothing in a workspace until they are added to it. Their role in that workspace is one of three.

| Role | View | Create and edit | Manage workspace members |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workspace Admin | Yes | Yes | Yes, for people whose organization role is lower |
| Workspace Member | Yes | Yes | No |
| Workspace Viewer | Yes | No | No |

The last column carries a second condition. A Workspace Admin manages the members of their workspace, and the organization role still applies on top, so they can change or remove only the people whose organization role is lower than their own.

Every Member and Viewer needs at least one workspace, and the invite form will not send without one. Someone with no workspace sees an empty product on sign-in, so check their row on **Settings > Members** first.

## When both apply

You hold an organization role and, in each workspace you belong to, a workspace role.

- Owner or Admin: Workspace Admin in every workspace, whether or not they were added to it
- Everyone else: the workspace role applies inside that workspace
- Member or Viewer with no membership: no access, and the workspace does not appear for them

Inside a workspace, the workspace role is the one that applies, in both directions. An organization Viewer added as Workspace Member can create, edit and share there. An organization Member added as Workspace Viewer cannot: Add Dataset, Add Row, Add Column, Run Prompt, Evaluate, Share and Create prompt are all greyed out for them.

One person can hold a different role in each workspace, and the difference is visible. In a workspace where you are a Workspace Member, a dataset gives you Add Row, Add Column, Run Prompt, Evaluate and Share. In a workspace where you are a Workspace Viewer, the same dataset greys all of those out and leaves you Compare and the read-only tabs.

A Workspace Admin cannot open organization settings or create a workspace. Both need an organization Admin or Owner. The role does give **Invite members** in the workspace switcher, the Members tab inside that workspace's own settings, and the **Build > Keys** page.

## What each role sees

The left sidebar and Settings are where a role difference shows first, and they follow different roles.

The **Settings** list follows your organization role, so it is identical in every workspace you switch to. Three things follow your role in the particular workspace instead, and appear and disappear as you switch: **Build > Keys** in the sidebar, **Invite members** in the switcher, and the tabs inside a workspace's own settings.

Every role sees the same left sidebar, with one exception. **Build > Keys** appears for Owners, Admins and Workspace Admins, and for nobody else.

The full grids are on the [permission reference](/docs/roles-and-permissions/reference/permissions).

## Annotation queue roles

[Annotation queues](/docs/annotations/concepts/queues-and-items) carry one more set of roles, granted per person per queue. They control what someone can do to the items in a queue. They do not control who can see the queue: anyone with access to the workspace can browse it.

| Queue role | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Annotator | Annotates the items assigned to them |
| Reviewer | Reviews other people's annotations and sends work back for rework |
| Manager | Manages the queue itself, its settings and its people |

Each person you add to a queue gets three independent checkboxes, one per role, so the roles combine freely. Someone needs both Annotator and Reviewer ticked to do both jobs. Whoever creates the queue is added with all three already ticked. The people you can add are the members of the workspace the queue is in.

<Note>
Inside a queue, the queue role applies instead of the workspace role. Someone with the Annotator role can annotate in that queue even if they cannot create or edit anything else in the workspace.
</Note>

Two settings on the queue change how the work is handed out:

- **Auto-assign items to all annotators.** With this on, every annotator is assigned every item and anyone can annotate any item
- **Submissions per item.** How many annotators must label each item, at most the number you have added

## Why it matters

The combination people get wrong is a team lead who cannot manage their own team. Making the lead an organization Member and a Workspace Admin looks right, and it fails the moment the people they lead are also organization Members, because a Workspace Admin can only act on people whose organization role is lower than their own. Put the people they lead in as organization Viewers and the same setup works. That one decision is the difference between a lead who runs their workspace and a lead who has to ask you for every removal.
