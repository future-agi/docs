---
title: "Read run results"
description: "Open a finished run, find the calls that failed, and read each one through its transcript, recording and verdicts"
---

A run's results page shows every call the run played, a **call** being one scenario played once, how each one was graded, and the patterns across them. This guide goes from failing calls to a single call, then to the whole run, and ends with what to do about what you find.

## Open the run

Open the environment, go to **Runs** and click a run. The header names the run, its status, the environment, how many tasks it played and when it started. If you came to find out why calls failed, **Debug failures** is the shortcut: it opens a diagnosis of the failing calls, covered [below](#let-the-run-be-diagnosed-for-you). The other actions beside it:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| Run evals | Grade this run's finished calls again, or with evaluations you added since; see [Add evaluations](/docs/environments/guides/add-evaluations#grade-a-run-that-already-finished) |
| Export | Download the run's results as a CSV file |

To run calls again, tick them in the table and use **Re-run**, covered [below](#re-run-selected-calls).

The page has two tabs: **Test runs**, with the call count, and **Analytics**.

## Find what failed

**Test runs** has one row per call. Calls are grouped by **Use case**, the job each scenario tests, and **Group by** regroups them by **Sub-goal**, **Accent**, **Age**, **Attack**, **Task** or **Status**, or turns grouping off with **No grouping**. Turn on **Expand all** to open every group, and filter with the chips above the table:

- **Failed**: the agent was measured and did not pass
- **Error**: the call could not run, such as a connection that never came up; it never counts against your agent
- **Inconclusive**: the call ran but was not evaluated; it never counts against your agent either
- **Passed**: the agent was measured and passed

While a run is still going, **Queued** and **In progress** count the calls that haven't finished.

Each row names the scenario and its trial, such as `check-refill-status-processing · Trial 2`, with its outcome and duration, and lists the scenario's sub-goals beneath it with a mark for each one that held. The columns are grouped:

- **Run details**: the use case the call belongs to
- **Scenario details**: the **Persona** the caller played, with their name, voice, age and traits, and the **Scenario**
- **System metrics**: **CSAT**, **Turns**, **Latency** and more, with values past a warning threshold flagged in red
- one column per evaluation, with the call's score. A cell with no score says why: **Failed**, **Timed out** or **Not scored**, with the reason when you hover it, and a dash when the evaluation doesn't apply to that scenario or the call never ran

Group rows show averages. **Columns** chooses which columns show, including each evaluation on the run.

## Read a single call

Click a row to open the call. Start with the call's **Evals** tab on the right: it gives each evaluation's verdict and the reason behind it, and the reason names what went wrong, such as a date of birth read back incorrectly. Then find that moment in the **Transcript** on the left, by its timestamp or its words.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results/voice-call-drawer.png" alt="A voice call drawer with the masked call ID, the Transcript and Traces tabs, the recording waveform and timestamped transcript on the left, and the Evals tab on the right with a failed evaluation expanded to show its reason" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Read the Evals reason, then find the moment in the transcript*


### The right of the drawer

The top holds the call's details, including its duration, average latency and **Ended**, the reason it ended, such as `silence-timed-out`. Below them are tabs:

- **Call Analytics**: timing, turns, talk split, silence, interruptions and speaking rate; [Call metrics](/docs/simulation/reference/call-metrics) defines each one
- **Evals**: each evaluation's verdict and its reason
- **Scenario**: the scenario the caller was playing, so you can check what the agent was asked to do
- **Messages**, **Logs** and **Attributes**: the call's raw messages, logs and attributes

### The left of the drawer

The **Transcript** shows the conversation turn by turn with timestamps, the silences between turns, interruptions, and every tool call the agent made with its arguments. When a tool call is involved in a failure, its arguments usually show the mistake directly. A voice call also has its **Recording** above the transcript, with the talk ratio between caller and agent and a filter for either side.

The tabs are **Transcript**, **Traces**, **Participants** and **World state**. **Traces** lists the traces your agent sent during the call, when it has [observability](/docs/environments/guides/build-an-environment#turn-on-observability) turned on; click one to open it. A call with none reads **No trace for this call**. **Participants** appears only when the call recorded a hand-off; see [Read a multi-agent call](#read-a-multi-agent-call). **World state** appears when [actors](/docs/environments/concepts/actors) are turned on, or when participants changed the same records.

Re-running a call adds a new **attempt** of that same call, inside its trial. Use the **Attempt** select to switch between attempts: the options read **Attempt N (latest)** and, for earlier ones, the attempt with the time of the re-run, such as **Attempt 1, re-run at 23 Nov 2026 2:02 PM**. Earlier attempts appear when the call itself was re-run, not only its evaluations, and the select lists the latest 10.

### World state

Every call starts from the scenario's starting state in the [world](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments#how-an-environment-works). As the call runs, your agent's tool calls and each actor's actions go through the world, which commits them or refuses them. **World state** shows the result: who attempted what, what was committed or refused, and how each record changed. The records and actions are your own agent's, so they differ for every environment. This tab is the call's own; the **World state** section of the **Contract** tab lists the world's tables as built.

Open **World state** when the transcript and the result disagree, for example when the agent says a change is done but the result says it failed.

1. Under **Calls into the world**, look at who attempted what. Your agent and each actor have a row, under **Participant**, with **Attempted** and **Committed** counts, plus **Refused** or **Faulted** when the world turned any away
2. Read the records that changed, with each field before and after. Turn on **Show N unchanged field(s)** to see the fields that stayed the same, and **Hide unchanged fields** to put them away
3. Check **Worth knowing** for anything about the call you should know before trusting its result
4. Use **Download end state** to save the world as the call ended

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results/world-state-tab.png" alt="The World state tab of a failed call in an example environment built for a sales team agent: Calls into the world shows the actor Jordan Taylor with one refused attempt, two records were added, and What the actors chose explains each action" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*World state shows what the agent and each actor did, and what the world refused. The records and actors are from an example environment*

**Before the run**, **What the actors chose**, **Approvals** and **Actors' model use** appear on this tab when the call has them. **Before the run** folds its messages behind **Show N message(s)** and **Hide**.

When there is nothing to read yet, the tab says why:

| Message | Meaning |
|---|---|
| This trial is still running: its World state shows here once it ends | The call has not finished |
| Nothing changed in the world in this trial | The call finished and no record changed |
| World state was not recorded for this trial | No World state exists for this call |
| This call's World state could not be loaded. Try again in a moment | The load failed; try again |


## Re-run selected calls

Tick calls in the **Test runs** table to run them again. Ticking a group's row ticks every call in it, and the box in the header ticks the page; a banner then offers **Select all** for every call that matches the current filter. The bar above the table counts the calls you ticked and the scenarios behind them.

Open **Re-run** and pick how to run them:

- **Re-run in this run** makes the ticked calls again inside this run. The new result becomes the call's latest attempt, and the earlier attempts stay under **Attempt** in the call drawer
- **Run as a new simulation** runs the scenarios behind the ticked calls as a new run, with the **Repeats** you set above it, which start at this run's trials. The new run opens when it starts, and appears on the **Runs** tab next to the one you re-ran, so the two can be compared directly

**Re-run** stays disabled until every call in the run has finished.

## Read the patterns

The **Analytics** tab summarizes the whole run. Its top cards are the quickest health check: **Calls passed**, **Drop-off**, **Response time p95**, **Cost / pass**, **CSAT satisfied** and **Change vs last run**. Below them, charts cover outcomes, latency, tools, and the slowest and most expensive calls. Of the charts, open these first:

- **Reliability across trials**: when a run has more than one trial, the pass rate with its 95% range, which tells you how much to trust it
- **Weakest scenarios**: the scenarios that failed most often
- **How calls ended**: every end reason from the platform, mapped onto one list

## Act on what you find

Every failing call has one of two causes: the agent did the wrong thing, or the test asked for the wrong thing. Telling them apart decides where the fix goes.

<Mermaid chart={`

flowchart TB
    F["Failed call"] --> E["Read the Evals reason,<br/>then the transcript at that moment"]
    E --> Q{"Did the agent break a rule,<br/>misuse a tool, or give a wrong answer?"}
    Q -->|"yes: the agent is wrong"| A["Fix the agent where it lives,<br/>on its platform or in its code"]
    A --> AG["Re-run the failing calls"]
    Q -->|"no: the test is wrong"| T["Fix the scenario or sub-goal<br/>through chat orchestration"]
    T --> TG["The next run tests the right thing"]

The agent is wrong when the transcript shows it breaking one of its own rules, calling a tool with the wrong arguments, or telling the caller something false. Fix it where it lives, then [re-run](#re-run-selected-calls) the failing calls, inside this run or as a new run to compare on **Runs**.

The test is wrong when the **Scenario** tab asks for something the agent was never meant to do, or a sub-goal checks the wrong thing. Fix it through [chat orchestration](/docs/environments/concepts/chat-orchestration), for example "this scenario expects a transfer, but the agent is allowed to answer opening hours itself", so the next run tests the right thing.

### Let the run be diagnosed for you

**Debug failures**, in the run's header, reads every call in the run and reports which goals your agent broke, the ways each one broke, and the calls behind each; [Debug failures](/docs/environments/guides/debug-failures) covers the drawer.

Once the diagnosis has finished, **Run Epoch** at the bottom of the drawer searches for a version of your agent that fixes the failing scenarios, and proves it before anything changes. [Run Epoch](/docs/environments/guides/run-epoch) covers it.

## Read a multi-agent call

When your agent hands work to other agents, a failed call needs one more question answered: which agent caused the result. Follow the call's hand-offs to the one that did. [Multi-agent apps](/docs/environments/concepts/multi-agent-apps) explains the coordinator and its participants, and the environment's **Contract** tab shows which participants your app declares.

### Open the call's Participants tab

Open a run, click a call, and choose **Participants** beside **Transcript** and **Traces**. The tab only appears on a call that has participants. It opens with a one-line summary of the call, such as `2 hand-offs to 2 participants. 2 finished.`, and a note on anything the record is missing. The summary and the problem lines are clickable, so click one to jump to the step it points at.

When the tab can't show the full sequence yet, it says why:

| Message | What to do |
|---|---|
| Collecting this call's participants… | The call is still being read; check back shortly |
| Couldn't load this call's participants | Reopen the call |
| This call's participants couldn't be read. Its record may have hand-offs that aren't shown | Treat the sequence as incomplete |
| No hand-off was recorded in this attempt | Nothing was handed over in this attempt |
| Stopped by the user during this call. What its participants did up to the stop is shown | The run was stopped mid-call; read what happened up to the stop |

### Find the agent at fault

1. Start from a problem: click a failed or refused step in the problem list at the top of the tab, or find the participant that did not finish in the status lines under the diagram, and click its step to jump to it
2. In the step detail, read who acted and what came back. The header names the participant and the tool, such as `<participant> → <tool>`. For example, **Refused by the world · not permitted** under a participant's tool call means that participant tried an action it isn't allowed
3. Read the participant's status line under the diagram, such as **Past their action deadline**, to see whether it also ran out of time
4. Check its **Participant checks** to see which of its own checks failed

The parts below cover each of these.

### Follow the sequence

The sequence diagram has one lane for the **Caller**, one for the **Coordinator** and one for each participant, and each row is a step.

Three controls change what you see:

- **All turns** shows every step
- **Fold tool calls** collapses tool calls into their parent step
- **Problems only** keeps the problems and the steps that led to them, and **Show all** brings everything back

To follow one participant, choose **Follow `<name>`**. **Stop following `<name>`** shows everyone again. Rounds that repeat fold into one row. Click it to see every round, and **Fold the repeated rounds** folds them back.

Click a step to open its detail, with **Show in transcript** and **Open in trace** to see the same moment elsewhere. **Copy a link to this step** gives you a link that opens the call at that exact step, and a **Link copied** toast confirms it, so you can share a failure with a teammate.

*Click a step to read who acted and what came back. The step is from an example travel concierge app*

### Read each participant's status lines and checks

Under the diagram, a status line names each participant that didn't finish cleanly:

- **Not observed:** a declared participant with nothing in the call's record
- **Took part, no end recorded:** it did something, but nothing says how it ended
- **Cut short:** it was stopped before it finished, for one of these reasons:
  - it was cancelled, or a cancel was requested
  - it went past its deadline
  - it never returned before the call ended
  - an infrastructure fault stopped it
- **Past their action deadline:** it acted after its time was up
- **Not declared:** it took part but your app doesn't declare it

**Participant checks** list each participant's own checks, graded on that participant's calls only. **Failed and unsure checks** places each problem on the step it belongs to, so you can jump to the moment.

### Compare trials and re-run attempts

When a scenario played more than once, the **Trial** select at the top of the drawer switches between its trials. **Show trials** lines them up step by step, with a **Result** column showing how each one ended, and **Hide trials** puts them away. **Split at** marks the step where the trials went different ways, such as one trial calling a tool the others never called, and **Show tool calls** reveals the tool calls on each route.

Re-running a call adds a new attempt of it inside the same trial. The **Attempt** select keeps the earlier attempts readable, and each attempt keeps its own participants' record.
