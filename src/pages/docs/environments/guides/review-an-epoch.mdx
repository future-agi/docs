---
title: "Review an epoch"
description: "Read an epoch's verdict and evidence, then ship its winner or decide what to try next"
---

A finished [epoch](/docs/environments/concepts/epoch) hands you its best candidate with the evidence for it, or tells you why there isn't one. The decision stays yours: your agent changes only when you ship the candidate.

An epoch headed **Attempt N from your coding agent** is a fix your own coding agent submitted. You read and ship it the same way. [Fix with your coding agent](/docs/environments/guides/fix-with-your-coding-agent) says how attempts work.

## Open an epoch

Open the environment and go to the **Epochs** tab. The tab appears once the environment has its first epoch, and lists every epoch, newest first:

- **Result** is the winner's pass rate on the held-back scenarios before and after the change, such as `Held back 0% → 75%`, with `none broke` or the number of must-pass scenarios it broke underneath. When nothing was held back, it gives the target pass rate instead
- **Hand-off** shows **Pull request** once you've opened one for the winner, or **Default** once its version is the environment's default

To narrow the list, use the status filter: **All statuses**, **Running**, or any ending, such as **Ready for review** or **Failed**. Click an epoch to open it.

*An epoch that's Ready for review and has no hand-off is waiting for you*

## Read the verdict

The header names the environment, which opens its **Epochs** tab, then the epoch, such as **Epoch 4**. Next to it, a link says where the epoch came from: **From issue** with the issue's title, or **From run** with the run, such as **Run 4**. When the issue or the run has since been deleted, it reads **From a deleted issue** or **From a deleted run**, without a link.

The **⋯** menu in the header has **Copy epoch ID**, **Download patch** (only when there's a winner) and **Export epoch**, which saves the epoch as a JSON file.

The top of the epoch's page sums up the winner in one sentence, such as `Held-back pass rate 0% → 75%. No must-pass scenario broke.` Below it, three numbers, then cards for what the epoch cost and how fast your agent replies with the winner:

- **Held back**: the pass rate on the held-back scenarios, which the epoch never saw while it searched, before and after the change
- **Target**: the pass rate on the scenarios it was tuned on
- **Overfit gap**: the points between the two. A large gap means the winner does much better on the scenarios it saw than on the ones it didn't

When nothing was held back, the sentence gives the target pass rate, and only the **Target** number shows. A rate pools every repeat of every scenario, so it isn't a count of scenarios; the **Scenarios** tab has those.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/review-an-epoch/epoch-ready.png" alt="Epoch 4 Ready for review with the headline Held-back pass rate 0% to 75%. No must-pass scenario broke, cards for Held back 0% to 75%, Target 0% to 90% and an Overfit gap of 15 pts, Checks 7 of 7 passed, and How it was decided, Review Candidate 4 and Ship in the header, and a Candidates tab with four candidates" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Read the held-back number first: it's the one the epoch couldn't tune for*

### The checks

**Checks**, under the numbers, counts the checks the winner passed, such as `Checks · 7 of 7 passed`. Click **Show all** to read each one:

| Check | What passing means |
|---|---|
| **Change is valid** | The change applies cleanly and edits only files the epoch may edit |
| **Target scenarios** | A clear gain on the scenarios it was tuned on |
| **Held-back scenarios** | A clear gain on the scenarios it never saw |
| **Must-pass scenarios** | None of the must-pass scenarios broke, including the ones you protected |
| **Holds over repeats** | The result held up when the scenarios ran again |
| **Reply time and cost** | The candidate stayed within the reply-time and cost limits you set, measured against your current agent |
| **Model unchanged** (**Approved models** when you allowed more than one) | The candidate called only the models you approved |

A check that ran but couldn't measure reads `Unknown`, with the reason and what would make it known. A check that didn't run, because an earlier one failed, says which one.

### Labels on the result

A label by the headline says when the result is proven on less than usual:

- **Limited validation**: one to three failing scenarios were held back, so the epoch can't tell whether the fix works on cases it never saw. A line under the label counts how many of the held-back scenarios the winner fixed
- **Proven on the targets only**: nothing was held back, so the winner is proven only on the scenarios it was tuned on

Other lines can appear under the verdict too. The page names any protected scenario that is flaky on your current agent, which the epoch couldn't hold the winner to. If you added [evaluations](/docs/environments/guides/add-evaluations), they score the winner after the epoch ends, and **Ship** waits until they finish. An evaluation the winner does worse on is listed, but it doesn't change the verdict.

## Compare the candidates

The tabs below the verdict show how the epoch got there:

- **Candidates**: candidates grouped by where each one stopped, so you can see which check stopped them. Each row opens the candidate's change and its run. The chart of each candidate's pass rate on the target scenarios stays below the groups
- **Changes**: the change a candidate makes to your agent, file by file
- **Scenarios**: each scenario's result, with what broke listed first. Use the chips **All**, **Target** and **Held back** to filter. Click a row to open a before and after view of that scenario's calls, and **Open call** to read one
- **Cost**: what the epoch spent, the hold kept for runs still going, and the budget

While the epoch runs, the diagnosis's causes show on the page. Once it ends, they fold into one **Diagnosis** line, which opens to the causes.

**How it was decided**, in the header, opens the evidence behind the pick: the winner's numbers, and every candidate's result on the target and held-back scenarios.

## Read the change

Click **Review Candidate 4** (the button names the winner, Candidate 4 in this example), or the **Changes** tab, to see the winner's change against the version the epoch started from. Each file shows its added and removed lines. **Download patch** in the **⋯** menu saves the change as a file.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/review-an-epoch/epoch-changes.png" alt="The Changes tab of Epoch 4 showing the diff of prompts/refill-line.md, which adds plain-language meanings for the ready, processing and on hold statuses, a line to name the pickup store, and a line to offer a pharmacist for questions about a dose, a side effect or an interaction" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Read the change the way you'd read a teammate's pull request*

## Ship the winner

Click **Ship**. A dialog says what will happen before anything does:

- for an agent connected from a source repository: `Open a pull request for Candidate 4?`, with the repository, the branch it opens into, and the files it changes. Click **Open pull request**
- for an agent connected as a code upload: `Make Candidate 4's version the default?`, with the version and the files it changes. Click **Make default**

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/review-an-epoch/ship-confirm.png" alt="The Open a pull request for Candidate 4 dialog showing the northwind/refill-line-agent repository, Into main @ 4c1e9a2, the one changed file prompts/refill-line.md with its added and removed line counts, and the Open pull request button" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Nothing reaches your repository until you confirm*

Once it's shipped, **Ship** turns into **View pull request**, or reads **Default**, and the epoch's **Hand-off** on the **Epochs** tab says the same. If the repository can't take the pull request, **Download patch** takes the place of **Ship**, so you can apply the change yourself.

A result with **Limited validation** or **Proven on the targets only** opens as a draft pull request, marked with that label in its title and body.

The pull request is written for a reviewer. Its title starts with `Epoch` and says what it changes: how many diagnosed causes it fixes, and in which file or how many files. A result with a strength label carries the label in the title too. The body has the checks and their results, what changed, why, and what could still go wrong, with a link back to the epoch. When the epoch started from [imported evidence](/docs/environments/guides/import-evidence), the body says where its cases came from.

## When an epoch ends without a winner

Every ending other than **Ready for review** leaves your agent as it was. Every ending uses the same layout: why the epoch ended, then one way on. That is **Retry Epoch 4** (with the epoch's own number) on a failed epoch you may retry, and **Run Epoch again** on any other ending. **Run Epoch again** opens from the issue the epoch came from, or the launch form when it didn't come from an issue. The page says what to do next:

| Ending | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **No safe improvement** | No candidate passed every check. The headline says which check stopped them, and **Show each candidate's result** lists each one | Read **What to try next** on the page: keep your current agent, see why each candidate was rejected, or click **Run Epoch again** with other cases or more parts it may change |
| **Could not reproduce** | The scenarios you picked didn't fail often enough on your current agent | Check that they still fail, then click **Run Epoch again** |
| **Stopped at budget** | The epoch reached its credit budget | Click **Run Epoch again** with a larger budget |
| **Stopped** | You clicked **Stop** | Click **Run Epoch again** when you're ready |
| **Too few failing scenarios** | The run had too few failing scenarios to work on | Pick more failing scenarios, or let the epoch write variations of them |
| **Failed** | The epoch couldn't finish | See below |

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/review-an-epoch/no-safe-improvement.png" alt="Epoch 3 No safe improvement with the headline saying no candidate passed every check, the What to try next list with See the candidates, How it was decided and Run a new epoch, and four rejected candidates, one stopped at each of Target scenarios, Must-pass scenarios and Reply time and cost" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*A rejected candidate tells you what doesn't work, which narrows the next epoch*

### When an epoch fails

A failed epoch says why, in one of four ways:

- it ran out of its time limit: click **Run Epoch again** with a longer time limit
- it couldn't start, because too many epochs were running: click **Retry Epoch** in a few minutes
- it stopped because of a problem on our side, not your agent: click **Retry Epoch**
- it stopped on something only you can change: the page says what to change, then click **Run Epoch again**

**Retry Epoch** starts a new epoch with the same settings and opens it, under the next number. You can retry a failed epoch once; after that, start a new one from [Debug failures](/docs/environments/guides/debug-failures) on the run, as [Run Epoch](/docs/environments/guides/run-epoch) describes.
