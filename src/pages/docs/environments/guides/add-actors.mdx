---
title: "Add actors"
description: "Add, edit and limit the people and systems around your agent on an environment's Actors tab"
---

[Actors](/docs/environments/concepts/actors) are the people and systems around your agent in a test: the caller, someone with them, a manager who approves. This guide checks the cast the builder proposed, adds or edits an actor, and sets the limits that keep actors from taking over a call.

<Note>
You need an environment in **Ready** status; [Build an environment](/docs/environments/guides/build-an-environment) covers getting one. If your environment has no **Actors** tab, actors aren't turned on for your deployment. On a self-hosted install, they're controlled by `HARNESS_ACTORS_ENABLED` in the [configuration reference](/docs/self-hosting/configuration/reference#agent-simulations-in-hosted-sandboxes).
</Note>

## Check the cast

Open the environment and go to **Actors**. The tab lists everyone the agent deals with who isn't the agent, in two groups: **On the caller's side** and **In the world**. Each row shows what the actor **Wants**, what it **May do** in the world, and when it **Comes in**. A row that reads **Comes in differently in 3 scenarios** has scenarios that bring it in at their own moment.

Click **What they see** on a row to check what that actor can see and what it **Never sees**. An actor never sees the material a call is graded on.

If the builder found a rule in your agent's material that it couldn't give to any actor, **Needs your decision** appears at the top. **Accept as written** keeps the cast as it is, and **Edit actor** opens an actor so you can give it that rule.

## Add an actor

Click **Add actor** and pick the side the actor sits on: **On the caller's side** for someone with the caller, or **In the world** for someone the agent reaches through its tools. The form opens with **Name**, a **Role** in a few words, such as a manager or a family member, and an **Id** made from the name. You can change the **Id** before the first save; after that it's fixed, so scenarios and past runs keep pointing at the same actor.

{/* SCREENSHOT TODO: add-actor-form.png. The Add actor form for a new actor In the world, Name "Dana Brooks", Role "store manager who approves refunds". Callout on "Add actor". Clean-capture rules apply */}

Then decide how the actor takes part:

- **How they join the conversation**: **Doesn't speak in the conversation**, **Heard nearby**, **Takes the phone**, **Phone menu** or **Writes in the chat**. The voice choices only apply to voice agents. For a voice actor, **How they sound** sets gender, age and accent, and **How loud, next to the caller** sets its volume
- **How they act in the world**: **Only the actions each scenario gives them**, **Decide their own actions when reached**, or **Both**
- **World actions they may take**: pick from the environment's own actions. The world refuses anything else the actor tries and records the attempt. If the environment's tools all run inside your agent, nobody in the world can act, and the form says so
- **Comes in**: when the actor first takes part, such as **Present from the start**, **When the agent calls an action** or **When the agent contacts them**. A scenario can give the actor its own moment
- **If they do not answer**: what happens when the world has waited long enough, such as **Nothing happens**, **They decline**, or another actor answering instead. **Waits, in seconds** sets how long; 0 waits as long as the call lasts

Give the actor what it knows. **What they know** takes facts one per line, such as "the refund limit is $500". **Files they can read** offers the environment's own documents, and **Records they can read** the world's record collections. A file the grader reads is never offered.

**More details** holds the actor's own aim (**What they want**, which isn't your agent's task), what it **May agree to**, what it **Keeps to themselves**, when it **Hands over**, when it **Is done**, and **How they behave**. The actor uses these whenever it speaks or decides. **Kind** is a label for what the actor is, such as a person or a system, and doesn't change how it behaves.

Click **Save to draft**. The actor joins the environment's unsaved changes and is saved with them.

## Edit or remove an actor

Click **Edit** on a row to open the same form. If an actor is part of scenarios, the form keeps the choices those scenarios depend on, such as how it joins, and says which scenarios hold it in place. **Remove** takes the actor out of the draft; saved versions keep it.

If someone else changes the actors while you're editing, the form loads their version with your edits kept and asks you to check them and save again.

## Set the limits

Open **Limits** on the tab. These settings stop actors from taking over a call, and a scenario can set its own values:

- **How many at once on a call**
- **Caller takes back after agent turns**
- **Seconds of actors per call**
- **Most messages in a conversation before the run**
- **Most turns each actor takes during a run** and **Most turns all actors take together in a run**
- **Model actors are played on**, and **The caller's model**

An empty field takes the default for the channel, and `none` means no limit. Click **Save limits to draft**.

## Check who can reach whom

**Who can reach whom** lists each actor's role, when it comes in, who brings it in, and which other actors its actions reach. Read it after a change to check that the cast still fits together.

If a run doesn't behave the way you expect, open the call's **World state** in [Read run results](/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results#world-state): it shows every action each actor tried, which ones the world refused, and what changed.
