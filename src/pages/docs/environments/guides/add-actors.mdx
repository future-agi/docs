---

Shape the cast of an environment on its **Actors** tab: review the [actors](/docs/environments/concepts/actors) the builder proposed, add or change them, and set their limits. Then, in a scenario, make one of your agent's tools time out, fail or need an actor's approval, to see how your agent copes. Jump to [Make a tool fail in a scenario](#make-a-tool-fail-in-a-scenario) for that part.

<Note>
You need an environment in **Ready** status; [Build an environment](/docs/environments/guides/build-an-environment) covers getting one. If the environment has no **Actors** tab, actors are not turned on for your deployment. See the [configuration reference](/docs/self-hosting/configuration/reference#agent-simulations-in-hosted-sandboxes).
</Note>

Every change on this tab waits in the environment's draft, with its other unsaved changes, until you save the environment. While you have unsaved actor changes, the tab shows **Unsaved changes: saved with the environment's other changes**.

## Check the cast

When actors are turned on, building the environment proposes a cast from your agent's own material, so the cast you find depends on your agent. Check it before you rely on it.

Open the environment and go to **Actors**, the third tab, right after **Contract**. Each row is one actor, grouped under **On the caller's side** or **In the world**; check who is in each group. **What they see** on a row lists what that actor can see and what it never sees. An environment with none reads **No actors yet. Add one, or ask the builder chat.** The builder chat is the environment's own chat panel, where you can ask for actors in plain words, such as "add a manager who approves refunds over $200".

*Actors sit on the caller's side or in the world. This example is from a sales team agent's environment*

If **Needs your decision** appears, a rule from your agent's material couldn't be given to an actor. Choose **Accept as written** to keep the cast as it is, or **Edit actor** to change the actor concerned.

Environments made from a template are locked: you can read the cast, but add, edit and remove are hidden and **Limits** is disabled.


To see which actors a scenario includes, open the scenario's details and read its **Actors** row.

## Add an actor

1. Click **Add actor** and pick **On the caller's side** or **In the world**
2. Fill in **Name**, optionally a **Role**, and the **Id**. **Brought in by** is optional
3. Under **How they join the conversation**, pick how the actor takes part. The choices follow your agent: a voice agent offers **Heard nearby**, **Takes the phone** and **Phone menu**, and a chat agent offers **Writes in the chat**. Choose **Doesn't speak in the conversation** if the actor should act only through the world's actions
4. Describe what the actor wants and may do. **What they want**, **May agree to**, **Keeps to themselves**, **Hands over when**, **Is done when**, **What they know** and **How they behave** all shape how they speak and decide
5. Under **How they act in the world**, choose how the actor acts, then pick **World actions they may take**. The list holds your environment's own world actions: the actions your agent's tools go through and changes to the world's records, so it differs for every agent. If a chosen action already belongs to another actor, an alert names the action and its current holder and says giving it here lets both take it. Tick **Both should have it** to let both actors take it
6. Set **Comes in** to say when the actor first takes part
7. Set **Waits, in seconds (optional)** to how long the world waits for the actor (0 waits as long as the call lasts), and **If they do not answer** to what happens then
8. Give it material to draw on: **Files they can read** and **Records they can read**
9. Click **Save to draft**. **Cancel** closes the form without saving

### Voice and advanced fields

Further down the form:

- **How they sound** and **How loud, next to the caller**: the actor's voice, for a voice agent
- **Model they are played on**: the model that plays this actor. Leave it to use the environment's default
- **Most turns in a call** or **Most lines in a call**, depending on how they join: this actor's own cap
- **Upload a document** under **Files they can read**: adds a file for the actor to read. An upload never replaces a file that already exists
- **More details**: anything else the actor should know

If an actor's description is too long, the form says so before you save.

## Edit or remove an actor

Click **Edit** on a row to open the form titled **Edit** followed by the actor's name. Click **Remove**, then confirm **Remove** followed by the name. The prompt reads **They leave the draft. Saved versions keep them.**

While a scenario includes an actor, you cannot remove it, and you cannot change how it joins the conversation.

## Set the limits

Click **Limits** to open them, and **Hide limits** to close them. Click **Save limits to draft** when done.

| Limit | Default | When it's reached |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How many at once on a call | 2 | An actor due to come in doesn't join |
| Caller takes back after agent turns | 4 | Actors stop taking turns and the caller carries the conversation |
| Seconds of actors per call | 60 | Counted from the first actor coming in. When it runs out, every actor stops speaking |
| Most messages in a conversation before the run | 20 | The conversation set up before your agent starts is cut at that many messages |
| Most turns each actor takes during a run | 10 | That actor stops answering |
| Most turns all actors take together in a run | 20 | No actor answers again |

In the last two labels, a run means one call. The first three apply to voice calls; a chat call doesn't enforce them, so don't rely on them there. Leave a field empty to use the default for the channel.

## Check who can reach whom

With two or more actors, **Who can reach whom** appears. Read it after a change to check the cast still fits together.

## Make a tool fail in a scenario

A **test condition** makes one of your agent's tools misbehave in one scenario. It is hidden from your agent, which is why the section is called **Hidden · test conditions**: your agent meets the failure the way it would in production, without warning. Use one to see how your agent copes when a tool times out, fails, is unavailable or needs an approval first. The call's [World state](/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results#world-state) then shows what the tool did.

<Note>
If the scenario editor has no **Hidden · test conditions** section, test conditions are not turned on for your deployment. See the [configuration reference](/docs/self-hosting/configuration/reference#agent-simulations-in-hosted-sandboxes).
</Note>

### Which tools can have a condition

A test condition is held by the test world, so it can only be set on a tool whose calls go through the test world. Which tools those are, and what they do, comes from your own agent:

- A tool that runs inside your agent can't be held. It is listed but greyed out
- An agent [hosted on a platform](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments#when-your-agent-runs-on-a-platform) gets no world, so none of its tools can have a condition
- **Runs, then the answer is lost** and **Needs approval first** are offered only for a tool that changes something in the world
- **Needs approval first** also needs an actor in the cast who may take another world action that can record the approval. Without one, the mode isn't offered

When actors are turned on, building the environment may also add test conditions to some scenarios. They show in the same section.

### Add a condition

1. Open the environment's **Scenarios** tab and click the pencil icon on the scenario's row to open **Edit scenario**, as in [Work with scenarios](/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios#edit-a-scenario)
2. Scroll to **Hidden · test conditions**. A scenario without any reads **Tools behave normally**
3. Click **Add a test condition**
4. Pick the **Tool**. If a tool is greyed out, it runs inside your agent, and hovering shows **This tool runs inside the agent, so it cannot be held**. Pick another tool
5. Pick **What happens**. It stays disabled until you pick a tool
6. Fill in the fields that mode asks for, then click **Add a test condition** under the fields. It stays disabled until a tool and a mode are picked and the fields are valid. **Cancel** beside it closes the form without adding

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/add-test-conditions/test-conditions-empty.png" alt="The Edit scenario panel scrolled to Hidden · test conditions, reading Tools behave normally, with the Add a test condition button" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*A scenario with no test conditions reads Tools behave normally*

Each mode does something different to your agent's call and asks for its own fields:

| What happens | What your agent meets | Fields it asks for | Ranges |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Times out before it runs** | The call waits, then times out. Nothing changes in the world | **On which call**, **Delay in seconds** | Call 1 or higher, default 1. Delay 0 to 30, default 30 |
| **Runs, then the answer is lost** | The world makes the change, then the agent hears a timeout. A retry makes it twice | **On which call**, **Delay in seconds** | Call 1 or higher, default 1. Delay 0 to 30, default 30 |
| **Unavailable** | The service answers that it is down | **On which call** | Call 1 or higher, default 1 |
| **Answers with an error** | The service answers with the status and message you set | **On which call**, **Status**, **Message** | Call 1 or higher, default 1. Status 400 to 599, default 500. Message up to 500 characters |
| **Needs approval first** | The world refuses the change until an actor records an approval of it | **Approval it needs**, **Approved when (optional)** | None |

For **Needs approval first**, **Approval it needs** lists the world actions your actors may take, each with who holds it, such as `<action>, by <actor>`. Pick the one that records the approval.

### Save the scenario

Click **Save scenario**. Conditions are checked when you save. To remove one, click the trash icon next to it.

### Fix a refused condition

A condition the test world cannot apply is refused when you save, with a message in this shape:

```text
Scenario X has a test condition the test world cannot apply: ...
```

The text after the colon says why. For **Needs approval first**, the tool must be one that changes something, and the scenario must include an actor who holds the approval action. Give an actor the approval action under **World actions they may take**, step 5 of [Add an actor](#add-an-actor) (or **Edit** an existing actor), then save the scenario again.

## Manage actors from MCP

The MCP server's **Simulation** tool group covers the **Actors** tab, in the order you'd use it:

- **Read the cast**: `list_environment_actors` lists an environment's actors, and `get_environment_actor` reads one
- **Change the cast**: `validate_environment_actor` checks an actor's fields before you add it, `create_environment_actor`, `update_environment_actor` and `delete_environment_actor` change the cast in the draft, and `set_environment_actor_limits` sets the limits. `accept_environment_actor_unresolved` accepts a point under **Needs your decision** as written
- **Save**: `list_environment_drafts` shows what waits in the draft, `review_environment_drafts` lists the changes, `validate_environment_drafts` proves them, and `save_environment_drafts` saves the validated versions

`validate_environment_drafts` spends credits, as **Validate** does in the platform. Turn the group on or off under **Settings** > **MCP Server** > **Tool Groups**; [Use the MCP server](/docs/falcon-ai/guides/use-the-mcp-server) covers connecting an assistant.
