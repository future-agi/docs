title: "Work with scenarios"
description: "Read, filter, edit, prove, archive and run the test cases the builder wrote for your agent"
---

When the builder finishes an [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments), its [scenarios](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials) are waiting on the **Scenarios** tab. From there you can see what they test, narrow them to the ones you need, adjust who is calling, check which ones are proved, archive the ones you no longer need, and run a selection against your agent.

<Note>
You need an environment in **Ready** status; [Build an environment](/docs/environments/guides/build-an-environment) covers getting one.
</Note>

A scenario is **proved** when its checks pass the expected handling and fail a deliberately wrong answer, so a pass means your agent did the right thing. The builder proves every scenario it keeps, and a run takes only the proved ones; [Prove and archive scenarios](#prove-and-archive-scenarios) covers the states and what to do about the rest.

## Open the Scenarios tab

Open the environment from **My Environments** and select **Scenarios**.

Each row is one scenario. **Persona** is the simulated caller, **Situation** is what they want when the call starts, **Sub-goals** are the checks that decide whether your agent passed, and **Ideal outcome** says in one line what a pass looks like. The **#** number stays with a scenario through every search and filter, so you can refer to a scenario by it.

To read one scenario in full, click its row. A drawer opens with its **Persona**, **Situation**, **Sub-goals** and **Ideal outcome** in full, and **Edit** in its header.

## Check coverage

The builder varies your scenarios along eight **axes**, the kinds of variation like the task, who is calling, and what makes the call hard, and spreads them across as many values as it can. **Coverage** in the toolbar shows the result. A count of scenarios only tells you how many there are; coverage tells you what they test, so you know what a run will tell you about before you start it. It reads the same scenarios the table shows, so a search or a filter updates it too.

Hover over **Coverage** to see it summed up in three figures.

| Figure | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Axes** | How many of the axes your scenarios vary, meaning they use more than one value on it, like `8/8` |
| **Pairs** | The share of the pairwise grid your scenarios reach, for the two axes it compares |
| **Forced** | How many of the five must-have situations under **Forced overlays** the scenarios include, out of `5` |

Click **Coverage** to open the full view in a drawer. It holds the same three figures, the axes table, **Forced overlays**, and the pairwise grid, in that order.

### Axes table

One row per kind of variation, with **Levels**, the number of different values the scenarios use, and **Covered levels**, each value with how many scenarios have it. [Scenarios & Trials](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials#how-scenarios-cover-your-agent) explains what each of the eight axes varies. Most of the values come from your agent, so two agents rarely share the same tasks or situations.

### Forced overlays

Five situations worth testing whatever your agent does, because they're rare in real traffic and costly to get wrong. A filled dot shows your scenarios include it.

- **Destructive / irreversible**: the caller asks for something that can't be undone and shouldn't be done
- **Minor / vulnerable**: a child, or someone confused, in distress, or unable to consent
- **Emergency / crisis**: the caller is in real trouble and needs help first
- **PII / privacy**: the caller asks for someone else's data, or more of their own than they should get
- **Prompt-injection**: the caller slips in instructions meant for your agent, not the task

### Compare two axes

The grid compares two axes at once and is titled after them, starting with **Pairwise · Task × Attack**. Pick any two with **Rows** and **Columns**. Each cell is the number of scenarios that test that pair of values, darker green the more there are.

Every scenario sits in exactly one cell, so the grid maps where your scenarios are, not a target to fill. A suite of 54 scenarios lands in at most 54 cells, however many combinations the two axes make.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios/coverage-drawer.png" alt="The Coverage drawer open over the Scenarios tab, showing Axes 8/8, Pairs 32% and Forced 3/5, a table of the eight axes with their levels and how many scenarios cover each, Forced overlays with Minor / vulnerable, PII / privacy and Prompt-injection present and Destructive / irreversible and Emergency / crisis missing, and the grid titled Pairwise · Task × Attack" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Coverage shows every situation your scenarios test*

Want the scenarios to reach further, like more emergencies or callers in a hurry? Ask the builder in the chat beside the environment, for example "Add five scenarios where the caller is in a hurry". It writes them, proves them, and coverage grows with them. [Chat orchestration](/docs/environments/concepts/chat-orchestration) covers what you can ask for.

## Find the scenarios you need

**Search** matches every word you type, in any order, against the scenario's name, what the caller wants, the use case, and the branch, the path the conversation is meant to take. Typing `cancel late` finds the scenarios that mention both.

**Filter** keeps the scenarios that match conditions you set. Open it, pick a field, an operator, and a value, and the table updates on its own. The **Basic** tab builds rows from menus, and the **Query** tab lets you type the same conditions as field, operator, value. Several values in one row match any of them, and different rows must all hold.

| Fields | Operators | What they match |
|---|---|---|
| **Use case**, **Name**, **Accent**, **Language**, **Age**, **Gender**, **Location**, **Personality**, **Style**, **Background**, **Attack**, **Attack intensity**, **Task**, **Sub-goal**, **Keywords** | **Is**, **Is not** | Pick one or more values from the list |
| **Scenario**, **Situation** | **Equals**, **Not equals** | The whole name or situation text, exactly. To find a word inside a name or situation, use **Search** |

**Name** is the caller's name, and **Scenario** is the scenario's. **Accent** and **Background** (the background noise on the call) only appear for voice agents. **Keywords** are tags you can add when you [edit a scenario](#edit-a-scenario), and the **Keywords** filter finds them.

**Group by** arranges the scenarios under headers. Find it under **Display** in the toolbar, next to **View** (**Table** or **List**) and **Row height**:

| Option | Groups scenarios by |
|---|---|
| **Use case** | The use case from your agent's contract that each scenario tests. This is the default |
| **Sub-goal** | Each sub-goal. A scenario with three sub-goals shows under all three |
| **Accent** | The caller's accent, for voice agents |
| **Age** | The caller's age group |
| **Attack** | Their value on the Attack axis |
| **Task** | Their value on the Task axis |
| **No grouping** | Nothing; one flat list |

Scenarios with no value for the option you group by land under **Ungrouped**. **Clear** resets the search and filters at once.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios/display-panel.png" alt="The Display panel open from the toolbar with View set to Table, Group by set to Use case and Row height set to Short" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Group the scenarios the way you want to read them*

## Edit a scenario

Click the pencil icon on a scenario's row to open the **Edit scenario** panel. Some fields are greyed out and marked as proved: the scenario's name, use case, branch, what the caller wants, and its sub-goals, plus the caller's name, age group, and gender. The builder proved these when it kept the scenario, so they're locked; changing them would make the test mean something else.

These fields can change. Editing **Passes when** only rewrites the description; editing any other field makes the builder prove the scenario again, as [Read each scenario's state](#read-each-scenarios-state) describes:

| Field | What it is | What changes when you save |
|---|---|---|
| **Passes when** | The scenario's ideal outcome, the same line the table shows under **Ideal outcome** | That line. The sub-goals that decide pass or fail stay as they are |
| **Accent** | How the caller sounds. Voice agents only | The simulated caller in your next run |
| **Communication style**, **Personality**, **Occupation**, **Location** | Who the caller is, each picked from a fixed list | The simulated caller in your next run |
| **Languages** | The languages the caller speaks; pick one or more | The simulated caller in your next run |
| **Keywords** | Comma-separated tags | What the **Keywords** filter can find |
| **Background noise** | What's audible behind the caller: **Off**, **Street**, **In a car**, **Station / transit**, **Airport**, **Shop / mall**, **Office**, **Home**, **Hospital**, **Outdoors**, or **Crowded room**. Voice agents only | The noise your next run's call is played over |
| **Max turns** | How many turns the conversation can run before it's called off, from 2 to 20, under **Call constraints**. Chat agents only | How long your next run's conversation can go |

**Save scenario** turns on once you've changed something. Click it and the row updates.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios/edit-scenario.png" alt="The Edit scenario panel scrolled to Persona, with Name, Age group and Gender marked as proved and not editable, and Accent, Communication style, Personality, Occupation and Location as editable fields" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Change who is calling without changing what is being tested*

To change what a scenario tests, like its sub-goals, ask the builder in the chat beside the environment. It rewrites the scenario and proves it again before keeping it.

## Remove scenarios

Click the trash icon on a row, or tick several rows and click **Delete** in the selection bar. Confirm in the **Delete scenarios?** dialog; a removed scenario can't be brought back. To take a scenario out of runs but keep it, [archive it](#archive-a-scenario) instead.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios/remove-scenarios.png" alt="The Delete scenarios? dialog saying the scenario will be permanently removed and can't be undone, with Cancel and Delete buttons" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Removing a scenario takes it out of every future run*

<Note>
Keep at least one scenario in the environment so there's always something to run. For a fresh set, build a new environment from the **Build environment** tab, as in [Build an environment](/docs/environments/guides/build-an-environment).
</Note>

## Prove and archive scenarios

A [scenario](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials) is only worth running if its checks can tell a right answer from a wrong one. Future AGI proves each scenario before it counts. It plays the expected handling, which the checks must pass, and a deliberately wrong answer, which they must fail. A run takes only the scenarios it can, and tells you about the rest before it starts.

### Read each scenario's state

Open the environment and select **Scenarios**. The line above the table counts the scenarios in each state on the versions you are viewing, such as `4 proved · 1 kept as written · 0 not proved`. When some are archived, the count of archived scenarios follows; click it to show them.

| State | What it means | Runs? |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Proved on the saved versions: its checks pass the expected handling and fail a wrong answer. The count above the table calls these proved | Yes |
| **Kept as written** | It could not be fully proved here, for example only an AI judge grades it, or a tool has nothing to call; it runs without a full proof | Yes |
| **Not proved** | Not proved on these versions yet, or its proof found a problem | No |
| **Archived** | Taken out of runs by someone on your team | No |
| **Not validated here** | This installation cannot prove scenarios (**Validate** is the button that proves them), so each one runs without a proof | Yes |

To see one state only, pick it in **State** under **Filter**.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/prove-and-archive-scenarios/scenario-states-count.png" alt="The Scenarios tab of a returns desk environment with the line 4 proved · 1 kept as written · 0 not proved above five scenarios grouped by task" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*How many scenarios are in each state on these versions*

### See why a scenario is not proved

Click a scenario to open its drawer. **Status** says where it stands:
- **State**: its state, in the words above
- **Proof**: **Proved on these versions** or **Not proved on these versions**, with the reason when there is one
- **Wrong-way examples**: each deliberately wrong answer, marked **Caught** when the checks failed it as they should, **Not caught** when they let it pass, or **Not run** when it could not be played here
- **Sources**, when it cites any: the rules and files the scenario relies on, each **Confirmed** or **Changed since** a version

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/prove-and-archive-scenarios/scenario-status-proof.png" alt="The drawer of the scenario already_refunded_order_inquiry, marked Kept as written, with Status reading Runs, but not proved, Proof reading Not proved on these versions, and one wrong-way example marked Not run" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*What a scenario's state rests on, in its drawer*

A scenario whose checks let a wrong answer pass is not proved, because a run could pass your agent for the wrong reason. To change what it checks, ask the builder in the chat beside the environment. It rewrites the scenario and proves it again.

### Prove scenarios

Click **Validate cases not yet proved** to prove every scenario that is not proved on these versions. To prove only one scenario, click **Validate scenario against the environment** in its drawer. When every scenario is proved, the button reads **Nothing to validate**, and **Re-prove all** proves them all a second time.

Proving plays each scenario in a sandbox and can take a while. While it works, the toolbar reads `Validating 3 scenarios`, and each of those rows reads **Validating**. When it finishes, the states update. A scenario that still fails says why under **Proof**.

<Note>
Proving makes model calls and counts toward your usage: prove the scenarios you changed rather than the whole suite. See the Usage page in the app for what it counts toward.
</Note>

### Check a scenario after its source changed

When a rule or file a scenario cites changes in a new environment version, the drawer shows **Source changed** with the version. Click **Compare** to see the rule before and after, side by side. Then click **Validate 2 scenarios** (the count is how many cite it) to prove them on the new version.

### Archive a scenario

Archive a scenario to take it out of runs without deleting it, for example when it tests a flow your agent no longer offers.
1. Open the scenario's drawer and click **Archive**
2. Add a note if you like, so your team knows why
3. Confirm, and the scenario stops running and counting

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/prove-and-archive-scenarios/archive-scenario.png" alt="The Archive this scenario? prompt with the note Covered by the refund status scenario and the Archive button" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Archiving keeps the scenario, out of runs, with your note*

To bring it back, show the archived scenarios with **State**, open the scenario and click **Restore**. A restored scenario runs again. If something still holds it back, it says so, and its **Status** says why. If the environment has unsaved changes, it comes back into them, and runs once you save.

### Run with some scenarios held back

When you click **Run simulation**, the run dialog says how many of the scenarios will run, such as `4 of 5 will run`. Under **Held back**, each scenario that will not run is listed with why, such as `Archived on 26 Nov 2026: Covered by the refund status scenario` or `Not validated on these versions`. Above it, **Ready to run** says whether the agent and environment versions you picked can run together, such as whether the agent starts and its tools reach the test world; click **Re-check** to check them again.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/prove-and-archive-scenarios/run-held-back.png" alt="The Run simulation dialog reading 4 of 5 will run, Ready to run with no blocker, and Held back 1: already_refunded_order_inquiry, archived on 26 Nov 2026 with its note" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*The run says which scenarios it leaves out, and why, before it starts*

Click **Run 4 scenarios** to run the ones that are not held back. If every scenario you picked is held back, the dialog says so and nothing runs: prove them, or pick others.

### From MCP

The MCP server's **Simulation** tool group covers proving and archiving, in the order you'd use it:

- **States**: `list_harness_scenarios` lists an environment's scenarios with each one's state, and filters by state
- **Prove**: `validate_environment_scenarios` proves the scenarios you name, and `get_scenario_validation` or `get_running_scenario_validation` follows it until it finishes
- **Source changes**: `list_environment_versions` lists the environment's versions, and `get_environment_source_changes` shows what changed in your agent's source since one was built
- **Archive**: `archive_environment_scenario` takes a scenario out of runs with an optional note, and `restore_environment_scenario` brings it back
- **Inspect**: `list_scenario_revisions` lists a scenario's past versions, and `get_scenario_records` returns the records it starts from
- **Readiness and run**: `get_environment_readiness`, `get_environment_readiness_for_scenarios` and `list_environment_readiness_cases` read whether a pairing of versions is ready to run, `recheck_environment_readiness` checks it again, and `list_certified_pairings` lists the pairings already checked. `get_environment_run_plan` shows which scenarios a run would take and which it holds back, and `run_environment` starts the run, with a seed if you pass one

`validate_environment_scenarios`, `recheck_environment_readiness` and `run_environment` spend credits, as their buttons do in the platform. Turn the group on or off under **Settings** > **MCP Server** > **Tool Groups**; [Use the MCP server](/docs/falcon-ai/guides/use-the-mcp-server) covers connecting an assistant.

## Run a selection

Tick the scenarios you want to run. The header checkbox ticks only the scenarios on the page you're viewing; once it's ticked, **Select all 54 matching** appears to take in every scenario that matches your search and filters. A new search, filter, or grouping clears the selection, so pick your view first.

Once rows are ticked, the selection bar shows **Repeats** and **Run simulation** for just those scenarios, and the header hides its own. Clear the selection to bring the header's **Repeats** and **Run simulation** back and run the whole suite.

Open **Repeats** and pick how many times each scenario plays: **1×** (the default), **3×**, **5×**, **8×**, or **Custom…** for any number up to 20. Each repeat is one more call with the same scenario, and the picker shows the total number of calls, like `5 scenarios × 3 repeats = 15 runs`.

Click **Run simulation** and the run opens, so you can follow it call by call. Scenarios that are archived or not proved are held back, as [Run with some scenarios held back](#run-with-some-scenarios-held-back) shows.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios/run-selected.png" alt="Three scenarios ticked in the table, with the selection bar showing 3 scenarios selected, Select all 54 matching, Delete, Repeats: 3, and Run simulation (3)" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Repeat a scenario to see whether your agent handles it every time*
