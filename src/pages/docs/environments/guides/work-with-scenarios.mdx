title: "Work with scenarios"

When the builder finishes an [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments), its [scenarios](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials) are waiting on the **Scenarios** tab. From there you can see what they test, narrow them to the ones you need, adjust who is calling, and run a selection against your agent.

<Note>
You need an environment in **Ready** status; [Build an environment](/docs/environments/guides/build-an-environment) covers getting one.
</Note>

## Open the Scenarios tab

Open the environment from **My Environments** and select **Scenarios**.

Each row is one scenario. **Persona** is the simulated caller, **Situation** is what they want when the call starts, **Sub-goals** are the checks that decide whether your agent passed, and **Ideal outcome** says in one line what a pass looks like. The **#** number stays with a scenario through every search and filter, so you can refer to a scenario by it.

To read one scenario in full, switch to **List** and click its name.

## Read the Coverage card

The builder varies your scenarios along eight **axes**, the kinds of variation like the task, who is calling, and what makes the call hard, and spreads them across as many values as it can. The **Coverage** card above the table shows the result. A count of scenarios only tells you how many there are; the card tells you what they test, so you know what a run will tell you about before you start it. It reads the same scenarios the table shows, so a search or a filter updates it too.

Collapsed, the card sums it up in three figures.

| Figure | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Axes** | How many of the axes your scenarios vary, meaning they use more than one value on it, like `8/8` |
| **Pairs** | The share of the **Pairwise grid** your scenarios reach, for the two axes it compares |
| **Forced** | How many of the five must-have situations under **Forced overlays** the scenarios include, out of `5` |

Click the card to expand it. It holds the axes table, **Forced overlays**, and the **Pairwise grid**, in that order.

### Axes table

One row per kind of variation, with **Levels**, the number of different values the scenarios use, and **Covered levels**, each value with how many scenarios have it. [Scenarios & Trials](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials#how-scenarios-cover-your-agent) explains what each of the eight axes varies. Most of the values come from your agent, so two agents rarely share the same tasks or situations.

### Forced overlays

Five situations worth testing whatever your agent does, because they're rare in real traffic and costly to get wrong. A filled dot shows your scenarios include it.

- **Destructive / irreversible**: the caller asks for something that can't be undone and shouldn't be done
- **Minor / vulnerable**: a child, or someone confused, in distress, or unable to consent
- **Emergency / crisis**: the caller is in real trouble and needs help first
- **PII / privacy**: the caller asks for someone else's data, or more of their own than they should get
- **Prompt-injection**: the caller slips in instructions meant for your agent, not the task

### Pairwise grid

The grid compares two axes at once, **Task** against **Attack** to start. Pick any two with **Rows** and **Columns**. Each cell is the number of scenarios that test that pair of values, darker green the more there are.

Every scenario sits in exactly one cell, so the grid maps where your scenarios are, not a target to fill. A suite of 40 scenarios lands in at most 40 cells, however many combinations the two axes make.

*Coverage shows every situation your scenarios test*

Want the scenarios to reach further, like more emergencies or callers in a hurry? Ask the builder in the chat beside the environment, for example "Add five scenarios where the caller is in a hurry". It writes them, proves them, and the Coverage card grows with them. [Chat orchestration](/docs/environments/concepts/chat-orchestration) covers what you can ask for.

## Find the scenarios you need

**Search** matches every word you type, in any order, against the scenario's name, what the caller wants, the use case, and the branch, the path the conversation is meant to take. Typing `cancel late` finds the scenarios that mention both.

**Filter** keeps the scenarios that match conditions you set. Open it, pick a field, an operator, and a value, and the table updates on its own. The **Basic** tab builds rows from menus, and the **Query** tab lets you type the same conditions as field, operator, value. Several values in one row match any of them, and different rows must all hold.

| Fields | Operators | What they match |
|---|---|---|
| **Use case**, **Name**, **Accent**, **Language**, **Age**, **Gender**, **Location**, **Personality**, **Style**, **Background**, **Attack**, **Attack intensity**, **Task**, **Sub-goal**, **Keywords** | **Is**, **Is not** | Pick one or more values from the list |
| **Scenario**, **Situation** | **Equals**, **Not equals** | The whole name or situation text, exactly. To find a word inside a name or situation, use **Search** |

**Name** is the caller's name, and **Scenario** is the scenario's. **Accent** and **Background** (the background noise on the call) only appear for voice agents. **Keywords** are tags you can add when you [edit a scenario](#edit-a-scenario), and the **Keywords** filter finds them.

**Group by** arranges the scenarios under headers:

| Option | Groups scenarios by |
|---|---|
| **Use case** | The use case from your agent's contract that each scenario tests. This is the default |
| **Sub-goal** | Each sub-goal. A scenario with three sub-goals shows under all three |
| **Accent** | The caller's accent, for voice agents |
| **Age** | The caller's age group |
| **Attack** | Their value on the Attack axis |
| **Task** | Their value on the Task axis |
| **No grouping** | Nothing; one flat list |

Scenarios with no value for the option you group by land under **Ungrouped**. **Clear** resets the search and filters at once.

## Edit a scenario

Click the pencil icon on a scenario's row to open the **Edit scenario** panel. Some fields are greyed out and marked as proved: the scenario's name, use case, branch, what the caller wants, and its sub-goals, plus the caller's name, age group, and gender. The builder proved these when it kept the scenario, so they're locked; changing them would make the test mean something else.

These fields can change, and the builder proves the scenario again when you edit one:

| Field | What it is | What changes when you save |
|---|---|---|
| **Passes when** | The scenario's ideal outcome, the same line the table shows under **Ideal outcome** | That line. The sub-goals that decide pass or fail stay as they are |
| **Accent** | How the caller sounds. Voice agents only | The simulated caller in your next run |
| **Communication style**, **Personality**, **Occupation**, **Location** | Who the caller is, each picked from a fixed list | The simulated caller in your next run |
| **Languages** | The languages the caller speaks; pick one or more | The simulated caller in your next run |
| **Keywords** | Comma-separated tags | What the **Keywords** filter can find |
| **Background noise** | What's audible behind the caller: **Off**, **Street**, **vehicle**, **Station / transit**, **Airport**, **Shop / mall**, **Office**, **home**, **hospital**, **outdoors**, or **crowd**. Voice agents only | The noise your next run's call is played over |

**Save scenario** turns on once you've changed something. Click it and the row updates.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios/edit-scenario.png" alt="The Edit scenario panel scrolled to Persona, with Name, Age group and Gender marked as proved and not editable, and Accent, Communication style, Personality, Occupation, Location, Languages and Keywords as editable fields" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Change who is calling without changing what is being tested*

To change what a scenario tests, like its sub-goals, ask the builder in the chat beside the environment. It rewrites the scenario and proves it again before keeping it.

## Remove scenarios

Click the trash icon on a row, or tick several rows and click **Delete** in the selection bar. Confirm in the **Delete scenarios?** dialog; a removed scenario can't be brought back.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios/remove-scenarios.png" alt="The Delete scenarios? dialog saying the scenario will be permanently removed and can't be undone, with Cancel and Delete buttons" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Removing a scenario takes it out of every future run*

<Note>
Keep at least one scenario in the environment so there's always something to run. For a fresh set, build a new environment from the **Build environment** tab, as in [Build an environment](/docs/environments/guides/build-an-environment).
</Note>

## Run a selection

Tick the scenarios you want to run. The header checkbox ticks only the scenarios on the page you're viewing; once it's ticked, **Select all 40 matching** appears to take in every scenario that matches your search and filters. A new search, filter, or grouping clears the selection, so pick your view first.

Once rows are ticked, the selection bar shows **Repeats** and **Run simulation** for just those scenarios. The **Repeats** and **Run simulation** in the header run the whole suite instead.

Open **Repeats** and pick how many times each scenario plays: **1×** (the default), **3×**, **5×**, **8×**, or **Custom…** for any number up to 20. Each repeat is one more call with the same scenario, and the picker shows the total number of calls, like `5 scenarios × 3 repeats = 15 runs`.

Click **Run simulation** and the run opens, so you can follow it call by call.

<img src="/images/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios/run-selected.png" alt="Three scenarios ticked in the table, with the selection bar showing 3 scenarios selected, Delete, Repeats set to 3, and Run simulation (3)" style={{ borderRadius: '5px' }} />
*Repeat a scenario to see whether your agent handles it every time*
