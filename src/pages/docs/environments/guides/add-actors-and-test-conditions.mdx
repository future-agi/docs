---
title: "Add actors and test conditions"
description: "Review and change the people around your agent, set their limits, and make a scenario's tool fail on purpose"
---

Shape who your agent deals with in its tests. On an environment's **Actors** tab, review the [actors](/docs/environments/concepts/actors) the builder proposed, add or change them, and set their limits. Then, in a scenario, add a **test condition** that makes one of your agent's tools time out, fail or wait for an actor's approval, to see how your agent copes; [Make a tool fail in a scenario](#make-a-tool-fail-in-a-scenario) covers that part.

<Note>
You need an environment in **Ready** status; [Build an environment](/docs/environments/guides/build-an-environment) covers getting one. If the environment has no **Actors** tab, actors aren't turned on for your deployment; see the [configuration reference](/docs/self-hosting/configuration/reference#agent-simulations-in-hosted-sandboxes). An environment made from a template is locked: you can read its cast, but adding, editing, removing and **Limits** are off.
</Note>

Every change on this page, to actors or to a scenario, goes into the environment's **unsaved changes** first, its draft. Runs keep using the saved version until you save, as in [Save your changes](#save-your-changes).

## Check the cast

Open the environment and go to **Actors**, the third tab, right after **Contract**. Each row is one actor, under one of two groups:

- **On the caller's side**: people with the caller, such as a partner who joins the chat
- **In the world**: people who act on your agent's data, such as a manager who approves refunds

On each row:

- **What they see** lists what the actor can see and what it never sees
- **Comes in** says when it first takes part
- The scenario count says how many scenarios include it

The cast depends on your agent's own material, so check it before you rely on it. An environment with no actors reads **No actors yet. Add one, or ask the builder chat.**

*Actors sit on the caller's side or in the world. This example is from a sales team agent's environment*

If **Needs your decision** appears, a rule from your agent's material couldn't be given to any actor. **Accept as written** keeps the cast as it is, so that rule isn't played by anyone in the tests; **Edit actor** opens the actor concerned so you can give it the rule.

With two or more actors, **Who can reach whom** lists each actor's role, when it comes in and whose records its actions reach. Read it after a change: an approver whose actions reach no one, for example, can never approve anything your agent asks for, so give it the action that records the approval.

## Add an actor

The quickest way is to ask the **builder chat**, the environment's own chat panel, in plain words, such as "add a store manager who approves refunds over $200". To add one yourself, follow the steps below; the values are a store manager's, as an example.

1. Click **Add actor** and pick **In the world**, since the manager acts on refunds rather than speaking with the caller
2. Fill in **Name** (`Dana Brooks`), **Role** (`Store manager`) and **Id** (`dana_brooks`). Name and Id are required, and some ways of joining also need a Role; the form marks what it needs. The Id is fixed once saved, so scenarios and past runs keep pointing at this actor
3. Under **How they join the conversation**, pick **Doesn't speak in the conversation**. A voice agent also offers **Heard nearby**, **Takes the phone** and **Phone menu**, and a chat agent offers **Writes in the chat**
4. Describe what the actor wants and how it decides. For Dana: **What they want**, "Approve refunds over $200 only with a written reason"; **What they know**, "The refund limit for the desk is $200". **May agree to**, **Keeps to themselves**, **Hands over when**, **Is done when** and **How they behave** shape the rest
5. Under **How they act in the world**, choose how the actor acts, then pick **World actions they may take**, such as the action that records an approval. The list holds your environment's own world actions, so it differs for every agent. If an action already belongs to another actor, an alert names its holder; tick **Both should have it** to let both take it
6. Set **Comes in**: **Present from the start**, **When the agent calls an action**, **When the agent says a word**, **From an agent turn**, **When the agent contacts them**, **At a set time**, **Once a record exists** or **By chance**. For Dana, **When the agent contacts them**
7. Set **Waits, in seconds (optional)**, how long the world waits for the actor (0 waits as long as the call lasts), and **If they do not answer**: **Nothing happens**, **They decline**, an action taken for them, or another actor answering instead
8. Give it material to draw on, if it needs any: **Files they can read** and **Records they can read**
9. Click **Save to draft**. **Cancel** closes the form without saving

### Voice and advanced fields

Further down the form:

- **How they sound** and **How loud, next to the caller**: the actor's voice, for a voice agent
- **Model they are played on**: the model that plays this actor. Leave it on the environment's model, or pick a cheaper one to cost less per actor turn
- **Most turns in a call** or **Most lines in a call**, depending on how they join: this actor's own cap
- **Upload a document** under **Files they can read**: adds a file for the actor to read. An upload never replaces a file that already exists
- **More details**: anything else the actor should know

If an actor's description is too long, the form says so before you save.

## Put an actor in a scenario

A scenario includes an actor when the builder puts it there, and a scenario's details list its actors on the **Actors** row. To add an actor to a scenario, or take one out, ask the builder chat, for example "include Dana Brooks in the refund-over-$200 scenario".

## Edit or remove an actor

Click **Edit** on a row to open the form titled **Edit** followed by the actor's name. Click **Remove**, then confirm **Remove** followed by the name. The prompt reads **They leave the draft. Saved versions keep them.**

An actor that a scenario includes can't be removed, and how it joins the conversation can't be changed. Clicking **Remove** then says why, such as **In 2 scenarios.** Take it out of those scenarios first, as in [Put an actor in a scenario](#put-an-actor-in-a-scenario).

## Set the limits

Click **Limits** to open them, and **Hide limits** to close them. Click **Save limits to draft** when done. A **run** in these labels means one call. Leave a field empty to use the default for the environment's channel, voice or chat.

| Limit | Applies to | Default | When it's reached |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| How many at once on a call | Voice | 2 | An actor due to come in doesn't join |
| Caller takes back after agent turns: after this many of your agent's turns, actors stop and the caller carries on | Voice | 4 | Actors stop taking turns |
| Seconds of actors per call | Voice | 60 | Counted from the first actor coming in. When it runs out, every actor stops speaking |
| Most messages in a conversation before the run | Voice and chat | 20 | The conversation set up before your agent starts is cut at that many messages |
| Most turns each actor takes during a run | Voice and chat | 10 | That actor stops answering |
| Most turns all actors take together in a run | Voice and chat | 20 | No actor answers again |

## Make a tool fail in a scenario

A **test condition** makes one of your agent's tools misbehave in one scenario. It is hidden from your agent, which is why the section is called **Hidden · test conditions**: your agent meets the failure the way it would in production, without warning. Use one to see how your agent copes when a tool times out, fails, is unavailable or needs an approval first. The call's [World state](/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results#world-state) then shows what the tool did.

<Note>
If the scenario editor has no **Hidden · test conditions** section, test conditions aren't turned on for your deployment. See the [configuration reference](/docs/self-hosting/configuration/reference#agent-simulations-in-hosted-sandboxes).
</Note>

### Which tools can be made to fail

Only tools that work on your agent's data from outside the agent can be made to fail, because the condition is played by the **world**, the test copy of that data, as the tool's call arrives:

- A tool that runs inside your agent's own code can't be held. It is listed but greyed out
- An agent [hosted on a platform](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments#when-your-agent-runs-on-a-platform) gets no world, so none of its tools can
- **Runs, then the answer is lost** and **Needs approval first** are offered only for a tool that changes something in the world
- **Needs approval first** also needs an actor who may take the action that records the approval. Without one, the mode isn't offered

When actors are turned on, building the environment may also add test conditions to some scenarios. They show in the same section.

### What each mode does

| What happens | What your agent meets | Fields it asks for | Ranges |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Times out before it runs** | The call waits, then times out. Nothing changes in the world | **On which call**, **Delay in seconds** | Call 1 or higher, default 1. Delay 0 to 30, default 30 |
| **Runs, then the answer is lost** | The world makes the change, then the agent hears a timeout. A retry makes it twice | **On which call**, **Delay in seconds** | Call 1 or higher, default 1. Delay 0 to 30, default 30 |
| **Unavailable** | The service answers that it is down | **On which call** | Call 1 or higher, default 1 |
| **Answers with an error** | The service answers with the status and message you set | **On which call**, **Status**, **Message** | Call 1 or higher, default 1. Status 400 to 599, default 500. Message up to 500 characters |
| **Needs approval first** | The world refuses the change until an actor records an approval of it | **Approval it needs**, **Approved when (optional)** | None |

For **Needs approval first**, **Approval it needs** lists the world actions your actors may take, each with who holds it, such as `<action>, by <actor>`; pick the one that records the approval. **Approved when (optional)** narrows which approvals count, by the field values the approval must hold, such as `{"status": "approved"}`. Leave it empty to accept any approval recorded by that action.

### Add a condition

1. Open the environment's **Scenarios** tab and click the pencil icon on the scenario's row to open **Edit scenario**, as in [Work with scenarios](/docs/environments/guides/work-with-scenarios#edit-a-scenario)
2. Scroll to **Hidden · test conditions**. A scenario without any reads **Tools behave normally**
3. Click **Add a test condition**
4. Pick the **Tool**. A greyed-out tool runs inside your agent, and hovering shows **This tool runs inside the agent, so it cannot be held**
5. Pick **What happens**, one of the modes above. It stays disabled until you pick a tool
6. Fill in the fields that mode asks for, then click **Add a test condition** under the fields. **Cancel** beside it closes the form without adding
7. Click **Save scenario**. Conditions are checked when you save, and the scenario waits in the unsaved changes with the rest. To remove a condition, click the trash icon next to it

### Fix a refused condition

A condition the world can't apply is refused when you save the scenario, with a message in this shape:

```text
Scenario X has a test condition the test world cannot apply: ...
```

The text after the colon says why. For **Needs approval first**, the tool must be one that changes something, and the scenario must include an actor who holds the approval action. Give an actor that action under **World actions they may take**, step 5 of [Add an actor](#add-an-actor), put that actor in the scenario, then save the scenario again.

## Save your changes

Actor changes, limits and scenario edits all wait in the environment's **unsaved changes** until you save them. The **Unsaved changes** bar under the version bar counts them.

1. Click **Review and save** to see every change before it is saved
2. Click **Validate**. Validating replays the scenarios against your agent with the changed cast, in a temporary copy of the environment, so it takes a few minutes and uses credits. **Save version** opens when it's done
3. Click **Save version**. The change becomes the environment's new default version, and the next run uses it

An environment that can't validate skips step 2 and saves straight away. If validation reports a problem, it names the scenarios that need a change; fix them and validate again. **Discard** throws the unsaved changes away and keeps the saved version.

## Manage actors from MCP

The MCP server's **Simulation** tool group covers the **Actors** tab, in the order you'd use it:

- **Read the cast**: `list_environment_actors` lists an environment's actors, and `get_environment_actor` reads one
- **Change the cast**: `validate_environment_actor` checks an actor's fields before you add it, `create_environment_actor`, `update_environment_actor` and `delete_environment_actor` change the cast in the draft, and `set_environment_actor_limits` sets the limits. `accept_environment_actor_unresolved` accepts a point under **Needs your decision** as written
- **Save**: `list_environment_drafts` shows what waits in the draft, `review_environment_drafts` lists the changes, `validate_environment_drafts` proves them, and `save_environment_drafts` saves the validated versions

`validate_environment_drafts` spends credits, as **Validate** does in the platform. Turn the group on or off under **Settings** > **MCP Server** > **Tool Groups**; [Use the MCP server](/docs/falcon-ai/guides/use-the-mcp-server) covers connecting an assistant.
