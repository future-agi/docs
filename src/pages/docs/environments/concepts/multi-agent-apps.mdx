## What a multi-agent app is

A **multi-agent app** is one where a coordinator hands parts of the job to participants. The coordinator is the agent the caller talks to. A returns desk might have a coordinator that talks to the customer, an order-lookup participant that finds the order, and a refund participant that issues the money back.

Tested as a single agent, such an app is a black box. A call fails and you can't tell whether the coordinator asked the wrong participant, a participant did the wrong thing, or nobody answered in time. An [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments) built from a multi-agent app keeps each participant separate, so a failed call points at the agent that caused it.

[Supported agents](/docs/environments/reference/supported-agents) are built with OpenAI Agents SDK, Google ADK or CrewAI.

## Declared and observed participants

Future AGI reads which participants your app declares from its source, and the environment's **Contract** tab lists them under **Participants**: "The agents this app hands work to, as its source declares them". Each one shows how it connects: on its **Own connection** or one that **Shares the app's connection**. A participant whose connection isn't known shows **Connection not declared**, and one that runs separately from the app shows **Runs on its own**.

A declaration isn't proof that a participant takes part. A run shows who actually did. A declared participant that never got any work in a call is marked **Not observed**, and one that took part without being declared is marked **Not declared**. A participant that took part but has no end recorded shows under **Took part, no end recorded**.

## What happens in a call

Every step of a call is one of four kinds: something said, a task handed over to a participant, a result sent back by it, or a tool call. Following them in order takes you from the caller's request to the answer, one participant at a time.

<Note>
Multi-agent testing covers a coordinator handing work to its participants one at a time, one level deep.
</Note>

## Each participant held to its own limits

Each participant has its own action deadline. A participant can be **Past their action deadline**, in which case the world refuses its later calls, or **Cut short**, when it was ended before it finished. These are the lines to read first when a call goes wrong.

Each participant can also have its own **Participant checks**, graded on that participant's calls alone and shown next to the call's own result. A refund participant can fail its check while the call as a whole passes, so a participant's own failure isn't hidden by a passing call.

Limits are held only when a participant runs on its own connection. When participants share the app's connection, the call says their limits weren't held.
