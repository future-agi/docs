## What a multi-agent app is

A **multi-agent app** is one where a coordinator hands parts of the job to other agents, its **participants**. The coordinator is the agent the simulated caller talks to. For example, a travel concierge app might have a coordinator that talks to the traveller, a pre-trip participant that prepares the briefing before a trip, and a booking participant that makes the reservations.

Tested as a single agent, such an app is a black box. A call fails and you can't tell whether the coordinator asked the wrong participant, a participant did the wrong thing, or nobody answered in time. An [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments) built from a multi-agent app keeps each participant separate, so a failed call points at the agent that caused it.

<Note>
Multi-agent testing covers a coordinator handing work to its participants one at a time, one level deep. An app with a single agent shows none of the participant views on this page.
</Note>

## What happens in a call

Every step of a call is one of four kinds: something said, a task handed to a participant, a result sent back by it, or a tool call. The call's **Participants** tab draws these steps in order, one lane per agent, from the caller's request to the answer. The tab appears only on a call that recorded a hand-off.

*The caller only ever talks to the coordinator. Each participant does its part and can be graded on it alone*

Each participant acts in the **world**, the test copy of your app's data that the call runs against, through the same tools your app's code gives it.

## Declared and observed participants

When the environment is built, Future AGI reads your app's source code and lists the participants the coordinator hands work to, such as the sub-agents it registers or calls. Those are the participants your app **declares**. The environment's **Contract** tab shows them on its **Participants** card: "The agents this app hands work to, as its source declares them". The card appears only when the app declares participants.

A declaration isn't proof that a participant takes part. Each call records the participants that actually did, and compares the two. The status lines sit under the sequence on the call's **Participants** tab:

| Status | What happened | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A participant's own row | Declared, and took part | Read its steps and its checks |
| **Not observed** | Declared, but the coordinator didn't hand it work in this call, or nothing about it reached the call's record | Expected when the scenario doesn't need it; otherwise check why the coordinator skipped it |
| **Not declared** | Took part, but your app's source doesn't list it | Check the **Participants** card, as in [Review the contract](/docs/environments/guides/review-the-contract), and rebuild if the build missed it |
| **Took part, no end recorded** | It was handed work, and no result came back | Open its hand-off step to see where it stopped |

## Each participant held to its own limits

When a multi-agent call goes wrong, the participant status lines are the first thing to read. Each participant has its own **action deadline**, the time it has to act, which the builder writes into each scenario along with the participant's own checks:

- **Past their action deadline**: the participant missed its deadline, so the world refused its later actions
- **Cut short**: the call ended it before it finished, with the reason, such as no return before the call ended, or a cancel
- **Participant checks**: graded on that participant's part of the call alone, and shown next to the call's own result. A booking participant can fail its check while the call as a whole passes, so its failure isn't hidden

These limits and checks hold only when the participant has its own **connection**, its own route from your app to the world. The **Participants** card on the **Contract** tab shows each one's connection:

- **Own connection**: limits and participant checks hold
- **Shares the app's connection**: its calls go through the coordinator's route, so the call says its limits weren't enforced and its own checks weren't graded
- **Connection not declared**: your app's source doesn't say
- **Runs on its own**: it runs in a process separate from the app. A participant past its deadline is also stopped, not only refused

To give a participant its own connection, have it read its tools' address from its own environment variable, separate from the coordinator's, then rebuild the environment.
