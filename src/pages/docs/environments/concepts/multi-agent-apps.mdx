## What a multi-agent app is

A **multi-agent app** is one where a coordinator hands parts of the job to other agents, its **participants**. The coordinator is the agent the caller talks to. For example, a travel concierge app might have a coordinator that talks to the traveller, a pre-trip participant that prepares the briefing before a trip, and a booking participant that makes the reservations.

Tested as a single agent, such an app is a black box. A call fails and you can't tell whether the coordinator asked the wrong participant, a participant did the wrong thing, or nobody answered in time. An [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments) built from a multi-agent app keeps each participant separate, so a failed call points at the agent that caused it.

<Note>
Multi-agent testing covers a coordinator handing work to its participants one at a time, one level deep. An app with a single agent shows none of the participant views.
</Note>

## Declared and observed participants

When the environment is built, Future AGI reads your app's code and lists the participants it finds there: those are the participants your app **declares**. The environment's **Contract** tab lists them under **Participants**: "The agents this app hands work to, as its source declares them". That card appears only when the app declares participants.

Each participant also shows how it connects:

- **Own connection**: the participant has a connection of its own. Its limits, below, are held
- **Shares the app's connection**: it uses the app's connection, and a call says its limits weren't held
- **Connection not declared**: the app's code doesn't say
- **Runs on its own**: it runs in a process separate from the app

A declaration isn't proof that a participant takes part. Each call records the participants that took part, which is what **observed** means here, and compares them with the declared ones:

| Declared | Took part in the call | Shown as |
|---|---|---|
| Yes | Yes | The participant, with its own checks and limits |
| Yes | No | **Not observed**: the coordinator didn't hand it work in this call, or nothing about it reached the call's record |
| No | Yes | **Not declared**: it took part, but your app's code doesn't list it, so check whether the app's code or the build missed it |

A participant that took part but has no end recorded shows as **Took part, no end recorded**.

## What happens in a call

Every step of a call is one of four kinds: something said, a task handed over to a participant, a result sent back by it, or a tool call. The call's **Participants** tab draws these steps in order, one lane per agent, from the caller's request to the answer. That tab appears only on a call that recorded a hand-off.

## Each participant held to its own limits

Each participant has its own **action deadline**, the time it has to act. Its actions go through the **world**, the test copy of your app's data that the call runs against. A participant can be **Past their action deadline**, in which case the world refuses its later actions, or **Cut short**, when it was ended before it finished. These status lines appear under the sequence on the call's **Participants** tab, and they are the first thing to read when a call goes wrong.

Each participant can also have its own **Participant checks**, graded on that participant's calls alone and shown next to the call's own result. A booking participant can fail its check while the call as a whole passes, so a participant's own failure isn't hidden by a passing call.

Limits are held only when a participant runs on its **Own connection**.

## Where multi-agent apps fit

- [Contracts](/docs/environments/concepts/contracts): the declared participants sit beside your agent's tools and rules
- [Scenarios & Trials](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials): each call in a scenario shows the hand-offs to participants
