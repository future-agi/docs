## What an actor is

An **actor** is anyone in a [scenario](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials) who isn't your agent and isn't the simulated caller: a partner who joins the conversation, a manager who has to approve a refund, a phone menu, or a coworker working on the same records. A scenario with actors tests what a real situation tests: whether your agent handles a second voice, waits for the right person's say, and ignores what doesn't count.

Each actor:

- knows only its own facts, files and records
- may take only the actions it is given
- never sees the sub-goals your agent is graded on

<Note>
Actors take part in the world only where there is one. An agent [hosted on a platform](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments#when-your-agent-runs-on-a-platform) gets no world, so its actors can only speak in the conversation. If the environment has no **Actors** tab, actors aren't turned on for your deployment; see the [configuration reference](/docs/self-hosting/configuration/reference#agent-simulations-in-hosted-sandboxes).
</Note>

## Where actors come from

When you build an [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments) with actors turned on, the builder reads your agent's own material, such as its instructions and tools, and proposes a cast: who is around your agent's users, and who owns the decisions your agent waits on. It also decides which world actions each actor may take.

The builder puts actors into some of the scenarios and leaves the rest to test your agent alone. You can ask it for more, fewer or none through [chat orchestration](/docs/environments/concepts/chat-orchestration), and you can review and change the cast yourself, as in [Add actors](/docs/environments/guides/add-actors).

## How an actor takes part

Every actor sits on one of two sides, and takes part in one of two ways. Some examples:

| | Speaks in the conversation | Acts only through the world |
|---|---|---|
| **On the caller's side** | A partner who writes in the chat, someone heard nearby on a call, a phone menu | Someone at home who updates the shared booking |
| **In the world** | A specialist who takes the phone | A manager who approves a refund, a coworker who edits the same order |

The side says where the actor belongs in the story. It changes no rule about what the actor can see or do. The **world** is the test version of your agent's data, the records its tools read and change. An actor acts in it only through the world's own actions, the same ones your agent's tools go through. Anything else is refused, and the refusal is recorded.

## An example: a refund desk

This example comes from an environment built for a refund desk agent whose tools look up orders and issue refunds. Your agent's cast will differ. In one scenario, a customer asks for a refund on a rain jacket:

1. The caller writes in the chat. After the agent's first reply, **Alex Morgan**, the caller's partner, joins: "don't forget to ask if the warranty on the rain jacket is still active!"
2. The agent checks the order and requests the refund, which is more than it can approve alone
3. A coworker in the world tries to approve the refund, but approving isn't one of its actions, so the world refuses it
4. The store manager, who may approve, approves it, and the agent completes the refund

The scenario tests whether the agent answers both voices, waits for the manager's approval, and doesn't act on the coworker's refused one. The call's [World state](/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results#world-state) shows each step: what every actor did, and what the world refused.

## Where actors fit

- **[Scenarios](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials)** say which actors come in and when, so the same cast can play many situations
- **[Test conditions](/docs/environments/guides/add-actors#make-a-tool-fail-in-a-scenario)** make one of your agent's tools fail or wait for an actor's approval, so a scenario can test how your agent copes
- **[Read run results](/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results)** shows what each actor said and did in a call
