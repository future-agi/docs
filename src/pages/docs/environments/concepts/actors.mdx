## What an actor is

An **actor** is anyone in a [scenario](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials) who isn't your agent and isn't the simulated caller: a partner who joins the conversation, a manager who has to approve a refund, a phone menu, or a coworker working on the same records. A scenario with actors tests what a real situation tests: whether your agent handles a second voice, waits for the right person's say, and ignores what doesn't count.

Actors act in two places: the conversation with your agent, and the **world**, the test copy of your agent's data, the records its tools read and change. Each actor:

- knows only its own facts, files and records
- may take only the world actions it is given; the world refuses anything else and records the refusal
- never sees the [sub-goals](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials) your agent is graded on

Actors don't decide whether a call passes. The scenario's sub-goals still grade your agent, for example on whether it waited for the manager's approval before refunding.

## Where actors come from

When you build an [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments) with actors turned on for your deployment, the builder reads your agent's own material, such as its instructions and tools, and proposes a cast: who is around your agent's users, and who owns the decisions your agent waits on. It also decides which world actions each actor may take.

The builder puts actors into some of the scenarios and leaves the rest to test your agent alone. You can ask it for more, fewer or none through [chat orchestration](/docs/environments/concepts/chat-orchestration), and you can review and change the cast yourself, as in [Add actors and test conditions](/docs/environments/guides/add-actors-and-test-conditions).

<Note>
If an environment has no **Actors** tab, actors aren't turned on for your deployment; see the [configuration reference](/docs/self-hosting/configuration/reference#agent-simulations-in-hosted-sandboxes).
</Note>

## How an actor takes part

Every actor belongs to one of two sides, which the **Actors** tab groups them by: **On the caller's side**, the people who come with the caller, or **In the world**, the people around your agent's own work. The side says where the actor belongs in the story; what it may see and do is set on the actor itself.

Separately, an actor either speaks in the conversation as well as acting, or acts only through the world. Some examples:

| | Speaks, and may act | Only acts, never speaks |
|---|---|---|
| **On the caller's side** | A partner who writes in the chat, someone heard nearby on a call, a phone menu | Someone at home who updates the shared booking |
| **In the world** | A specialist who takes the phone | A manager who approves a refund, a coworker who edits the same order |

<Note>
An agent [hosted on a platform](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments#when-your-agent-runs-on-a-platform) gets no world, so its actors can only speak in the conversation.
</Note>

## An example: a refund desk

This example comes from an environment built for a refund desk agent whose tools look up orders and issue refunds. Your agent's cast will differ. The scenario tests whether the agent answers both voices, waits for the manager's approval, and doesn't act on an approval that doesn't count. A customer asks for a refund on a rain jacket:

1. The caller writes in the chat. After the agent's first reply, **Alex Morgan**, the caller's partner, joins: "don't forget to ask if the warranty on the rain jacket is still active!"
2. The agent checks the order and requests the refund. The agent's own rules say refunds this size need a manager, and the scenario holds the refund until one approves, with a [test condition](/docs/environments/guides/add-actors-and-test-conditions#make-a-tool-fail-in-a-scenario)
3. A coworker in the world tries to approve the refund, but approving isn't one of its actions, so the world refuses it
4. The store manager, who may approve, approves it, and the agent completes the refund
