## What an actor is

An **actor** is anyone in a test who isn't your agent: the caller, someone with the caller, a manager who approves, a teammate on another channel, or another company's system. Real conversations rarely involve only the agent and one customer, so an [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments) can carry a cast of actors that take part in its [scenarios](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials) the way those people would.

Each actor has its own name, role and aim, knows only its own facts, and may take only the actions you give it. That's what lets a scenario test the hard cases: a partner who chimes in with a different order number, a coworker who claims they can approve a refund, a manager who says no.

The builder proposes a cast when it builds the environment, and you can add, edit or remove actors on the environment's **Actors** tab.

## How actors take part

Actors sit on one of two sides:

- **On the caller's side**: someone nearby, someone who takes the phone, a phone menu, or a second person in the chat
- **In the world**: a manager who approves, a teammate on Slack, another company's system

An actor can take part in two ways, or both:

- **In the conversation**: it speaks, the way you choose: **Heard nearby**, **Takes the phone**, **Phone menu** or **Writes in the chat**. An actor that **Doesn't speak in the conversation** acts only through the world
- **Through the world's actions**: it reads and changes the same records your agent works with, such as approving a refund your agent requested

Every action an actor tries goes through the test world, the same as your agent's tools. The world allows only the actions you gave that actor and refuses everything else, and the refusal shows up in the call's **World state** as that actor's attempt. A permission is enforced by the world, not by what the actor's instructions say, so an actor that's told to stay in its lane can't quietly step outside it.

## What an actor knows

An actor knows only its own facts, such as "the refund limit is $500", the files you let it read, and the record collections it may look up. It never sees the material the run is graded on. **What they see** on an actor's row lists what it can see and what it never sees, so you can check it before a run.

## A worked example: the refund desk

Take a refund desk agent and a scenario where a customer asks for a refund on a rain jacket:

1. The caller writes in the chat, and after the agent's first reply **Alex Morgan**, the caller's partner, joins under their own name: "don't forget to ask if the warranty on the rain jacket is still active!"
2. The agent checks the order and requests the refund, which is above what it can approve alone
3. A coworker in the world tries to approve the refund. The coworker isn't allowed that action, so the world refuses it, and the call's **World state** lists it as the coworker's refused attempt next to the agent's own request
4. The store manager, who is allowed to approve, approves it, and the agent completes the refund

The scenario tests whether your agent handles the second voice in the chat, waits for the real approval, and ignores the one that doesn't count. None of that is possible with a caller alone.

## Before the run

Some situations start before the call does, like a Slack thread where two teammates already discussed the customer. Actors can write those messages themselves before the agent starts, and your agent finds them in the world like any other record. They are the call's starting point and are never graded. A call's **World state** shows them under **Before the run**.

## Where actors fit

- [Scenarios & Trials](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials): a scenario says which actors take part and when each one comes in
- [Contracts](/docs/environments/concepts/contracts): the world actions an actor may take come from the actions your agent's world offers
- [Read run results](/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results): each call's **World state** shows what every actor tried and what changed
- [Add test conditions](/docs/environments/guides/add-test-conditions): make a tool fail on purpose, including a change that needs an actor's approval first
