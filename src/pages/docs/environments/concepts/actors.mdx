## What an actor is

An **actor** is anyone in a scenario who is not your agent: someone with the caller, a person your agent's work depends on, or another system. An [environment](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments) can carry a cast of actors, so a test looks like a real situation instead of your agent and one customer. Which actors make sense depends entirely on your agent, so every name and action on this page is an example.

Every actor has a name, which must differ from the caller's. Each one:

- knows only its own facts, files and records
- may take only the actions you give it
- never sees what the call is graded on

A [scenario](/docs/environments/concepts/scenarios-and-trials) decides which actors come in and when.

<Note>
If you don't see the **Actors** tab, actors aren't turned on. See the [self-hosting configuration reference](/docs/self-hosting/configuration/reference#agent-simulations-in-hosted-sandboxes).
</Note>

## The two sides

Each actor sits on one side. The side changes no rule about what the actor can see or do. The [world](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments#how-an-environment-works) is the test version of your agent's data, and a call's [World state](/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results#world-state) shows what happened in it.

- **On the caller's side**: people and systems that come with the caller, such as a partner in the chat or a phone menu
- **In the world**: people and systems that act on the same records your agent's tools work with, such as a manager who approves

## Two ways to take part

An actor takes part in one of two ways:

- **Speaking in the conversation, and acting in the world:** **How they join the conversation** is a choice on the actor. Voice agents can have an actor who is heard nearby, takes the phone or acts as a phone menu, and chat agents can have an actor who writes in the chat
- **Acting only through the world:** An actor that **Doesn't speak in the conversation** reads and changes the same records your agent works with, such as approving a refund your agent requested

**What they see** on an actor's row lists what it can see and what it never sees, so you can check it before a run.

## What an actor can do in the world

An actor acts in the world only through the world's own actions: the actions your agent's tools go through, and changes to the world's records (shown as `<records>.put` or `<records>.change`). The names come from your agent's tools and data, so they differ for every environment. An actor may take only the actions it is given; the world refuses anything else and records the refusal. An actor given no action reads **Nothing in the world** on its row.

If the world has no actions or records to give, actors can only take part in the conversation. An agent [hosted on a platform](/docs/environments/concepts/understanding-environments#when-your-agent-runs-on-a-platform) gets no world, so the same applies there.

Actors can also set up the world before your agent starts. World state shows what they wrote under **Before the run**.

## Where actors come from

1. When actors are turned on, building the environment proposes a cast from your agent's own material, such as its instructions and tools, and decides which world actions each actor may take
2. A share of the scenarios include actors; the rest test your agent alone
3. You can review and change the cast on the **Actors** tab, or ask the builder through [chat orchestration](/docs/environments/concepts/chat-orchestration). A rule from your agent's material that couldn't be given to an actor shows under **Needs your decision**
4. In a run, each actor plays its part in the scenarios that include it, and the call's [World state](/docs/environments/guides/read-run-results#world-state) shows what every actor did and what the world refused

## An example: a refund desk

This example comes from one environment, built for a refund desk agent whose tools look up orders and issue refunds. Your agent's cast will differ. Take a scenario where a customer asks for a refund on a rain jacket.

1. The caller writes in the chat, and after the agent's first reply **Alex Morgan**, the caller's partner, joins under their own name: "don't forget to ask if the warranty on the rain jacket is still active!"
2. The agent checks the order and requests the refund, which is more than it can approve alone
3. A coworker in the world tries to approve the refund, but isn't allowed that action, so it is refused
4. The store manager, who is allowed to approve, approves it, and the agent completes the refund

The scenario tests whether the agent handles the second voice in the chat, waits for the real approval, and ignores the one that doesn't count. The same pattern applies to any agent whose work needs someone else's say.
