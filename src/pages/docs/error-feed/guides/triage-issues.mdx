---
title: "Triage issues"
description: "Narrow, scan, and close out issues in the Error Feed list."
---

The Error Feed list can grow long fast, and most of it isn't what you need to look at today. This guide covers cutting it down to the issues that matter, reading the table quickly, and closing issues out, one row or many at a time.

## Narrow the list

The filter bar above the table gives you a search box and five selects: time range, project, status, severity, and fix layer.

Search (labeled "Search errors") matches against the issue title, so typing part of an error name narrows straight to it. Status and severity are the two triage axes covered on [Severity & Status](/docs/error-feed/concepts/severity-and-status); fix layer is the Prompt, Tools, Orchestration, or Guardrails label explained on [Understanding Error Feed](/docs/error-feed/concepts/understanding-error-feed). A **Clear** button appears next to the selects the moment any filter is active, and resets all of them in one click.

If you're starting a session cold, filter status to **Escalating** first: that's the bucket holding everything nobody has triaged yet, and it's the highest-signal place to begin. For the full list of options on every select, see [Issue fields & filters](/docs/error-feed/reference/issue-fields).

## Scan the table

Once the list is narrowed, each row is one issue, an issue [cluster](/docs/error-feed/concepts/understanding-error-feed), with eight columns: Error, Severity, Status, Events, Users, Fix Layer, Trend (14d), and Last seen.

Two of them are what you're actually deciding on: Severity tells you how bad it is, Status tells you where it stands in triage. Events and Users give you the blast radius, total occurrences versus distinct people who hit it, and Trend (14d) shows whether the issue is getting more common or fading. Last seen tells you if it's still happening at all.

Below the table, **Results per page** switches between 10, 25, and 50 rows, and **Back** / **Next** step through pages.

## Act on issues

You can close issues out three ways.

**In bulk.** Tick the checkbox on any row and a bulk-select toolbar appears. Its **Bulk actions** menu lets you mark every selected row as Resolved, Acknowledged, For Review, or Escalating in one action, which is the fastest way to clear a batch of duplicates or false positives.

**One at a time, from the header.** Open a row and use the **Resolve** or **Acknowledge** buttons in the issue's header. This is the quick path when you've already decided what to do and don't need the [full investigation](/docs/error-feed/guides/investigate-an-issue).

**One at a time, from the sidebar.** The metadata sidebar on the same page has Status, Severity, and Assignee rows, each of which opens a menu. Assignee's menu is headed "Assign to" and lists your org's members plus an Unassign option; before anyone is set it just reads "Assign".

<Warning>
None of these changes show a success or failure toast, they save silently, so trust the state on screen after you make a change rather than waiting for a confirmation. Assigning someone to an issue also sends no notification of any kind, so if it's urgent, tell them yourself.
</Warning>
