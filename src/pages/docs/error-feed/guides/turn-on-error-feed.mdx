---
title: "Turn on Error Feed"
description: "Raise a project's sampling rate so Error Feed starts scanning traces and issues begin to appear."
---

Error Feed doesn't scan a single trace until you tell it to. A new project ships with scanning off, so before anything shows up in the feed you need to raise one setting and give it a few minutes to run.

## Before you start

- Your workspace needs the Error Feed capability. Without it, the feed shows an upgrade message instead of a table, and the API answers with a 402.
- The project's sampling rate ships at 0, meaning nothing gets scanned. You have to raise it yourself.

## Raise the sampling rate

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Observe project">
    Go to the project you want Error Feed to scan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the configure drawer">
    Open the project's configure drawer, the same screen where you set up tracing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the sampling rate">
    Find the sampling control and raise it above 0. The rate is a percentage of incoming traces to scan, from 0 to 100.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Save the change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait">
    Give it a few minutes. Scanning runs on new traces as they arrive, not on a backlog.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
The rate only applies to traces that arrive after you save it. Nothing already scanned or already skipped gets reprocessed.
</Note>

## Choosing a rate

Sampling is a coverage-versus-cost call: scan more traces and you catch more, but you pay to scan more.

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|-----------|
| Setting up a project, or actively debugging | Keep the rate high so you see everything while you're paying attention |
| Steady production traffic | Keep it moderate, enough to catch recurring issues without scanning every trace |
| Chasing one specific, hard-to-reproduce error | Raise it temporarily for the duration of the investigation, then bring it back down |

## What "wait" means

Scanning kicks off once a trace's root span completes, and it pauses about ten seconds first before sampling and scanning it. If your traces arrive through Future AGI's collector rather than the inline path, they skip that trigger entirely and instead wait on a periodic sweep, which picks them up in small batches rather than right away. Either way, the first **issues** you see are built from **findings** the scan produces, so a fresh project with a newly raised rate can take a few minutes before its first row appears.
